Market Intelligence — Netherlands 31 AUG 2026

Mapping the EPR landscape: hospital groups and standalone hospitals across the Netherlands

The Dutch hospital EPR market is unusually consolidated for a country this size: ChipSoft’s HiX platform now sits behind the large majority of Dutch acute hospitals, Epic holds the academic medical centers and one entire regional alliance, and a live legal dispute between ChipSoft and three north-eastern hospitals over a proposed shared Epic rollout — resolved on appeal only in April 2026 — is reshaping the competitive map in real time. This report maps supplier positions site by site, with full address and postcode detail, as the baseline entry for FHI’s Netherlands database.

FHI Intelligence | Research & Data Collection Briefing | Updated 31 August 2026

Key findings

  • ChipSoft’s HiX is the default EPR across the Dutch hospital sector, mapped at 55 of 77 acute sites researched (10 directly confirmed, 45 assumed per the M&I/Partners 2024 market inventory) — including four of the eight university medical centers and the majority of general hospitals.
  • Epic holds the remaining academic footprint and one Santeon sub-alliance: both Amsterdam UMC sites, UMCG, Radboudumc and Maastricht UMC+ (won from Cerner/SAP in 2023), plus OLVG and St. Antonius Ziekenhuis within the seven-member Santeon teaching-hospital alliance — whose remaining five members run ChipSoft HiX.
  • A ChipSoft-vs-UMCG legal dispute over a proposed shared Epic rollout to Ommelander Ziekenhuis Groningen and Treant Zorggroep (three sites, currently on NEXUS) went against UMCG at first instance in 2025, then was reversed on appeal by the Gerechtshof Arnhem-Leeuwarden on 22 April 2026, clearing the joint rollout without a fresh EU tender — a decision with implications well beyond this one region for how Dutch hospitals can extend existing EPR contracts to partner organisations.
  • LUMC delayed a planned HiX upgrade in 2026 after a ChipSoft security/data incident, illustrating supplier-concentration risk: with ChipSoft behind the large majority of Dutch hospitals, an incident at the vendor has system-wide implications rather than being contained to one site.
  • Three sites carry confirmed data gaps — Meander Medisch Centrum and Gelre ziekenhuizen (Apeldoorn) are missing a confirmed postcode, and Slingeland Ziekenhuis (Doetinchem, part of Santiz) is missing both address and postcode — flagged here for direct follow-up rather than left unstated.

1. National context: consolidation, mandated data exchange and a market under active litigation

The Dutch hospital landscape comprises eight university medical centers (UMCs) — the academic tier that combines specialist care, research and teaching — alongside roughly 70 general (STZ/SAZ-classified) hospitals, many of which have merged into multi-site organisations over the past two decades. Unlike Ireland’s hospital-group structure, Dutch hospitals largely procure and govern their EPR contracts independently or through voluntary alliances rather than a single national commissioning body: there is no equivalent of Ireland’s HSE running a single national EHR tender for the acute sector. The market is consequently vendor-led rather than programme-led, and the resulting picture is one of a small number of platforms competing hospital by hospital and, increasingly, group by group.

Two structural forces shape how that competition plays out. First, the Wegiz (Wet elektronische gegevensuitwisseling in de zorg), in force since 1 July 2023, is the first Dutch law to mandate electronic data exchange between named categories of care provider, moving the market from voluntary standards (MedMij, the national LSP switchpoint) toward compliance-driven interoperability [2]. Second, the government’s VIPP subsidy programmes, administered via DUS-I, have funded incremental digitisation across five phases; VIPP 5 concluded at the end of 2024 [1]. Neither programme mandates a specific EPR vendor, but both raise the cost of running a poorly-integrated legacy system, which is part of what is now driving hospitals like Zuyderland off older platforms.

Seven teaching hospitals — not university medical centers, but STZ-designated teaching hospitals with a shared clinical and digital agenda — formed the Santeon alliance: Canisius Wilhelmina Ziekenhuis (Nijmegen), Catharina Ziekenhuis (Eindhoven), Maasstad Ziekenhuis (Rotterdam), Martini Ziekenhuis (Groningen), Medisch Spectrum Twente (Enschede), OLVG (Amsterdam, two sites) and Antonius Zorggroep (Nieuwegein/Utrecht, two sites) [4]. Santeon members share clinical-outcomes benchmarking but not a common EPR: five run ChipSoft HiX and two — OLVG and Antonius Zorggroep — run Epic, a split that makes Santeon a useful microcosm of the national market rather than a counter-example to it.

The competition authority ACM published a market study into hospital information systems and digital data exchange, flagging switching costs and supplier concentration as competition concerns in the Dutch hospital IT market [3]. The single most important structured data source behind this report’s general-hospital coverage is M&I/Partners’ 2024 market inventories of EPD (EPR) and PDMS (critical-care) suppliers across Dutch hospitals [5][6] — a named, dated, third-party survey, but not an official disclosure from the hospitals themselves, which is why the large majority of general-hospital records below are marked “assumed” rather than “confirmed”.

🔍 Supplier Watch — Epic vs. ChipSoft at the academic tier

Epic holds five of the nine academic-tier deployments mapped here, including the two most recent competitive wins — UMCG (2016, now central to the regional-EPD dispute) and Maastricht UMC+ (2023, taken from Cerner/SAP). 9. Data gaps, methodology and recommended next steps

This report was built from a single research pass across hospital and supplier websites, named Dutch health-IT trade press (Skipr, ICT&health, Zorgvisie, Computable, Dutch IT Channel, Medisch Contact, Digital Health News), the ACM’s market study, DUS-I/VIPP and Wegiz programme documentation, and the M&I/Partners 2024 EPD and PDMS market inventories, gathered on 31 August 2026. It is a strong first layer for the Netherlands database but has four structural gaps FHI should close before treating any record as complete:

  • The M&I/Partners inventory drives the large majority of general-hospital records. 45 of 55 ChipSoft positions, 6 of 15 Epic positions, and 3 of 7 NEXUS positions, plus all 6 critical-care positions, are marked “assumed” on this single third-party source rather than independently confirmed. A hospital-by-hospital outreach pass (or the next annual M&I/Partners release) is the natural way to convert these to confirmed status.
  • Three sites have confirmed address/postcode gaps. Meander Medisch Centrum (Amersfoort) and Gelre ziekenhuizen, locatie Apeldoorn are missing a postcode; Slingeland Ziekenhuis (Doetinchem, part of Santiz) is missing both address and postcode. These are flagged in the database rather than backfilled with an unverified figure.
  • The UMCG/OZG/Treant Epic-extension rollout is not yet confirmed live. The April 2026 appeals ruling cleared the legal path, but this report found no evidence the technical migration at Ommelander Ziekenhuis Groningen or Treant’s three sites has begun; all four are still recorded here on NEXUS pending confirmation of a go-live date.
  • Bed counts and staff numbers were not collected in this pass. Unlike the Ireland report, no comparable national bed-capacity dataset was located within the time available; this is a candidate addition for the next research cycle, alongside ownership-structure detail for the handful of independent (non-STZ) hospitals.

10. Conclusions

The Dutch EPR market looks, on its surface, like a stable two-and-a-half-vendor equilibrium: ChipSoft dominant, Epic entrenched at the largest academic centers, NEXUS a shrinking niche player. But two live developments this year show how quickly that equilibrium can move, and both deserve standing entries in FHI’s monitoring pipeline rather than one-off notes.

The UMCG/OZG/Treant dispute: what an Epic-extension win means for contract-modification precedent.

UMCG proposed extending its own 2015/2016 Epic contract to cover Ommelander Ziekenhuis Groningen (Scheemda) and the three Treant Zorggroep sites (Emmen, Hoogeveen, Stadskanaal), all then running NEXUS. ChipSoft challenged the move as an unlawful “material modification” of a public contract that should instead have triggered a fresh EU-wide tender — and won at first instance, with the Court of Noord-Nederland halting the plan and imposing a penalty on UMCG [12]. On 22 April 2026, the Gerechtshof Arnhem-Leeuwarden appeals court reversed that ruling, finding that UMCG’s original 2015 contract already permitted other hospitals to join on the condition that they fully cover their own usage costs — clearing the joint rollout to proceed without a new tender [13].

The practical effect, once the technical migration happens, is four more hospitals moving from NEXUS to Epic and a shrinking of NEXUS’s already-small Dutch footprint to just Meander, Bernhoven and Groene Hart (all three currently “assumed” rather than confirmed). But the more consequential effect is precedent: Dutch public hospitals now have appellate-level confirmation that an existing EPR contract can be extended to a partner organisation without a fresh competitive tender, provided the original contract contemplated it and the joining party pays its own way. FHI expects this ruling to be cited by other Dutch hospital groups weighing a shared-EPR extension over a full re-procurement, and will track any such follow-on extensions as a standing entry in the database.

Separately, LUMC’s decision to delay a planned HiX upgrade in 2026 following a ChipSoft data/security incident is a smaller story individually [15][14], but it is a useful reminder of what ChipSoft’s 55-of-77 market share actually means operationally: an incident at a single supplier now has potential system-wide implications across the large majority of Dutch acute hospitals, in a way that would not be true in a more fragmented market. FHI will track ChipSoft-specific incident disclosures as a distinct risk-monitoring line in the database going forward, separate from ordinary procurement and go-live tracking.
