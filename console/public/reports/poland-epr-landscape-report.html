Poland's full hospital census: 1,452 entities mapped, a thin but real confirmed-vendor evidence layer

This report supersedes this database's earlier representative ~44-group pass on Poland with a full national census: all 1,452 RPWDL-registered hospital-type entities, drawn directly from Poland's official healthcare-provider registry (Rejestr Podmiotow Wykonujacych Dzialalnosc Lecznicza). Poland's governance structure is genuinely the opposite of Hungary's: no body owns or mandates hospital IT nationally -- the Ministry of Health sets policy, NFZ pays but cannot own providers, and Centrum e-Zdrowia runs national interoperability only -- so every entity procures its own HIS/EPR system independently. Structurally mapping the full census is this report's central achievement; it is deliberately honest that the confirmed-vendor evidence layer remains thin -- 26 deployment records across 26 sites, 1.8% of the census -- carried forward unchanged from the earlier pass's TED procurement sweep and vendor/trade-press research, with Asseco Poland (AMMS/InfoMedica) the clear leader among confirmed deployments, followed by KAMSOFT, Konsultant IT / Nexus Polska (HIS ESKULAP), CloudiMed and CompuGroup Medical (CGM CLININET). A national-recovery-fund-driven digitisation wave -- Poland's KPO (Krajowy Plan Odbudowy) Action D1.1.2 -- is the confirmed driver behind a run of large HIS/EDM contracts at otherwise routine small and mid-size hospitals across 2025-2026, Poland's equivalent macro narrative to Hungary's OKFO mandate, arriving through fragmented, entity-by-entity procurement rather than a single national platform.

1. National context: a fragmented, entity-by-entity procurement market with no single owning or mandating body

Poland's Ministry of Health sets national health policy and owns a handful of institute-level hospitals directly, alongside separate hospital networks run by the Ministries of Defence and the Interior. NFZ (Narodowy Fundusz Zdrowia), the sole public payer, is organised into 16 voivodeship branches but is legally barred from owning or operating providers -- a payer only, with no equivalent to Hungary's OKFO or Czechia's kraj-ownership function. Centrum e-Zdrowia (CeZ), the national eHealth agency, operates 50+ central IT systems built around the P1 platform (e-recepta, e-skierowanie, IKP) -- national interoperability and citizen-facing services, not hospital-level HIS/EPR provision.

Ownership of the census's 1,452 entities splits several ways, visible directly in the RPWDL legal-entity-type codes each entity is registered under (see §4): voivodeship self-governments (marshal offices) own many of the larger regional/specialist hospitals; powiat (county) self-governments own the bulk of general district hospitals (243 confirmed nationally); the Ministries of Health, Defence and the Interior each own a smaller number of institute-level, military or uniformed-services hospitals directly; municipalities own some hospitals in larger cities; and a large population of privately registered entities -- from major chains such as Grupa LUX MED down to small single-site LLCs and sole proprietorships -- makes up the majority of the census by raw entity count. No single body owns or mandates a HIS/EPR platform across any part of this structure: each entity procures independently, subject only to CeZ's interoperability requirements and NFZ's billing-system integration.

One regional exception is worth naming precisely, because of what it is not: Malopolska voivodeship runs MSIM (Malopolski System Informacji Medycznej), a regional medical-data-exchange platform that hospitals integrate their own, separately-procured HIS into. This is an interoperability layer, not a shared or mandated HIS -- the reason this report's national map (§3) encodes census coverage density rather than a vendor claim, and keeps all vendor differentiation at the marker level for the 26 confirmed deployments.

2. Census methodology: from a representative sample to a full national census

This database's first Poland pass (12 September 2026) deliberately covered a representative ~44-group subset -- the largest and most vendor-confirmed hospital groups -- given Poland's scale (GUS's last full census, end-2016, counted 957 general stationary hospitals nationally, roughly 10x Hungary's 84-institution roster). That scoping decision was explicit and agreed with FHI leadership at the time, not a silent shortcut.

This pass replaces the sample with Poland's official national healthcare-provider registry: RPWDL (Rejestr Podmiotow Wykonujacych Dzialalnosc Lecznicza), filtered to hospital-type entities with active registration status as of 12 September 2026 -- 1,452 entities, each individually recorded with its RPWDL ledger number, registered address (used to derive the voivodeship/district breakdown in §5), legal-entity-type code (§4) and ownership type. A small number of synthetic placeholder groups from the earlier pass -- generic entries for LUX MED, Scanmed, Nowy Szpital, EMC and Medicover representing private chains as a whole, rather than real RPWDL entities -- were dropped from the census in favour of those chains' actual individual RPWDL-registered sites, several of which are visible in the census under their own names (see §7's note on Grupa Nowy Szpital).

The earlier pass's 26 confirmed HIS/EPR vendor deployments -- sourced from a 159-notice TED contract-award sample (CPV 48814000, 2024-2026) plus named trade press and vendor/hospital own-page disclosure -- were reconciled onto the new census records by matching each deployment's hospital name and city against the census (diacritic- and case-insensitive matching, then manual review of every match). This pass did not re-run the TED sweep or search for further vendor confirmations beyond that reconciliation: extending the confirmed-vendor evidence layer across more of the 1,452-entity census is explicitly flagged as the priority for a follow-up pass, not attempted here.

4. Ownership and legal structure of the census

Of the 1,452 entities, 829 (57%) are privately owned and 623 (43%) publicly owned, by RPWDL legal-entity-type code. The single most common legal form is the private limited-liability company (LET_13, 504 entities, 34.7% of the census) -- a category spanning everything from single-site specialist clinics to large private hospital chains. The traditional public SPZOZ form (LET_1 plus its alternate code LET_20) accounts for 451 entities (31.0%), and a further 124 entities (8.5%) are "commercialised" former-SPZOZ hospitals now registered as local-government-owned limited liability companies (LET_14) -- still publicly owned despite the company structure. 9. Data gaps and methodology honesty

This report is explicit about what the census build does and does not establish. It establishes, with high confidence, which 1,452 entities exist, where each is registered, and each entity's legal/ownership form -- drawn directly from Poland's official provider registry rather than inferred or sampled. It does not establish a HIS/EPR vendor for the vast majority of those entities: only 1.8% of the census carries a confirmed or named deployment record, and bed counts are not sourced for any of the 1,452 sites (0% coverage) -- a materially wider gap than this database's typical partial bed-count coverage for other countries, and an explicit priority for a follow-up pass rather than an oversight to gloss over.

The RPWDL registry itself has known limitations for this purpose: it registers legal entities, not necessarily distinct physical hospital buildings, so this census should be read as an entity-level model rather than a confirmed one-entity-per-physical-site count (the same simplification this database's earlier passes on smaller countries generally did not need to make explicit, given their scale). A small number of entities (3) carry no parseable legal-entity-type code and are shown as "unclassified" in §4 rather than guessed at.

11. National health-IT governance: bodies and current leadership

Poland's hospital-IT-relevant governance is genuinely dispersed: national policy (Ministry of Health) and national interoperability (Centrum e-Zdrowia) sit apart from hospital ownership (voivodeship and powiat self-governments, plus ministries and municipalities), and the sole payer (NFZ) is legally barred from owning providers at all. No regional or national body owns or mandates hospital-level HIS/EPR the way Hungary's OKFO or Czechia's kraje do. The table below sets out the bodies FHI will track as the national layer of the Polish market.

Ministerstwo Zdrowia

Ministry of Health

Sets national health policy and owns a small number of institute-level hospitals directly. The Ministry of Defence and Ministry of the Interior each separately own their own hospital networks (military and uniformed-services hospitals) -- no standalone hospital-ownership body equivalent to Hungary's OKFO exists in Poland.

NFZ

Narodowy Fundusz Zdrowia (National Health Fund)

Sole public payer, organised into 16 voivodeship branches. Legally prohibited from owning or operating healthcare providers -- a payer only, structurally distinct from Hungary's OKFO or Czechia's kraj-ownership model.

Centrum e-Zdrowia (CeZ)

National eHealth agency (est. 2000, formerly CSIOZ), operates 50+ central IT systems. Flagship: the P1 platform underpinning e-recepta (e-prescriptions), e-skierowanie (e-referrals) and IKP (Internet Patient Account). Runs national interoperability only -- no role in hospital-level HIS/EPR procurement. Named leadership: Maciej Wardaszko, Jeremi Olechnowicz, Anna Kurczewska-Formela, Jaroslaw Sot, Dorota Motyka, Andrzej Sarnowski.

Voivodeship self-governments

Urzedy marszalkowskie

Own many of the larger regional/specialist hospitals across Poland's 16 voivodeships, and are also the RPWDL-registered address unit this report's census breakdown (§5) is aggregated by. Some run shared regional medical-DATA-EXCHANGE platforms (e.g. Malopolska's MSIM) -- interoperability layers, not shared/mandated HIS platforms.

Unlike Austria's Lander or Czechia's kraje, no voivodeship in Poland has been confirmed to mandate a single HIS vendor across its owned hospital estate.

Powiat self-governments

Powiat (county) self-governments

Own the bulk of general district hospitals -- 243 powiat-owned hospitals confirmed nationally (2025 source).

12. Conclusions

Poland is now the largest fully-mapped market in this database by entity count: 1,452 RPWDL-registered hospital-type entities, individually recorded with legal form, ownership and voivodeship, replacing the earlier representative ~44-group pass. This is a genuinely different kind of achievement from that earlier report -- structural coverage of an entire national market, delivered for the first time -- and it should be read alongside an equally genuine limitation: the confirmed-vendor evidence layer carried forward from the earlier pass covers only 1.8% of the census, and bed counts are not yet sourced at all. For FHI's commercial purposes, the practical implication is that this report is an accurate structural map with a thin but real vendor-intelligence layer on top -- Asseco Poland's leading confirmed position, the KAMSOFT/Nexus Polska/CloudiMed challenger field, and the KPO-funded 2025-2026 contract wave are all well-evidenced findings, but extending vendor confirmation across meaningfully more of the census is the clear priority for the next pass, not a claim this report already makes.

Watch item: a follow-up pass should prioritise a fresh, wider TED sweep against the full census (rather than the 44-group sample the original 159-notice sweep was matched against), Sygnity's actual client list for the acquired Comarch HIS business, CloudiMed's specific product name, and named vendor confirmation for the nine Grupa Nowy Szpital entities identified in §7.
Watch item: bed-count sourcing is a wholesale gap (0 of 1,452 sites) -- a materially larger gap than any other country in this database and a priority to close incrementally as further sources (hospital annual reports, NFZ contract-value data as a bed-count proxy) are identified.
Watch item: given the KPO Action D1.1.2 funding wave is still active through 2026, a repeat TED sweep in 6-12 months would very plausibly convert several "unconfirmed" Konsultant IT relationships into confirmed ones, and surface further CloudiMed-style vendors not yet visible to general web search.
