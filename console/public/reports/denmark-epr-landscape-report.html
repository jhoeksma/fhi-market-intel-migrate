Mapping the EPR landscape: hospital groups and standalone hospitals across Denmark

Denmark runs the most regionally clean-cut EPR market of any Nordic country: exactly five regions, exactly two vendors, and no unmapped acute site. Epic’s locally-branded Sundhedsplatformen covers the whole east (Hovedstaden and Sjælland, 32 sites); Systematic’s Columna CIS platform covers the whole west under three separate regional brand names — EPJ SYD, MidtEPJ and NordEPJ (37 sites). But that tidy picture sits on top of a national reorganisation already under way: a 2024 health reform is merging Hovedstaden and Sjælland into “Region Østdanmark” from 1 January 2027, the Prime Minister is publicly pushing for a single national EPJ system, and a live 2026 citizen petition is calling for Sundhedsplatformen to be scrapped outright. This edition adds hospital-bed capacity (enabling a market-share-by-beds read alongside the site-count view), a country key-facts cover page, per-region hospital-location maps, and the national health-IT governance bodies to watch, as the baseline entry for FHI’s Denmark database.

Key findings

  • Denmark splits cleanly along a single fault line: Epic in the east, Systematic in the west. Epic’s Sundhedsplatformen covers all 32 mapped public sites across Region Hovedstaden and Region Sjælland; Systematic’s Columna CIS platform covers all 37 mapped public sites across Region Syddanmark (EPJ SYD, 16 sites), Region Midtjylland (MidtEPJ, 8 sites) and Region Nordjylland (NordEPJ, 13 sites) — a 100% regional-boundary match with no exceptions found in this research pass.
  • By mapped hospital beds, the Epic/Systematic split is closer than the 32/37 site count suggests. A bed count was confirmed for 50 of 85 sites (≈8,450 beds, with the balance either genuinely unconfirmed, a combined group-level figure that cannot be split by site, or a confirmed zero at outpatient-only sites) — see §7 for the full market-share-by-beds table and its coverage caveats.
  • Region Syddanmark evaluated and rejected Sundhedsplatformen/Epic in its 2017–2018 EU tender for EPJ SYD, choosing Systematic’s Columna CIS instead — a direct head-to-head loss for Epic in a market it otherwise dominates in eastern Denmark, and go-live proceeded in three regional waves from October 2021 to May 2022.
  • The 2024 sundhedsreform is merging Region Hovedstaden and Region Sjælland into “Region Østdanmark” from 1 January 2027 — both regions already run the same Epic/Sundhedsplatformen platform, so the merger is organisationally significant but should not itself force an EPR migration, unlike a cross-vendor regional merger would.
  • Denmark’s national health-data governance is itself being restructured in parallel. Sundhedsdatastyrelsen and MedCom both cease to exist as independent bodies on 31 December 2026, merging with sundhed.dk into a new body, Digital Sundhed Danmark, from 1 January 2027 — whose permanent leadership has not yet been named. See §9 for the full governance picture.
  • An active 2026 citizen petition (borgerforslag) calls for Sundhedsplatformen to be abolished across Region Hovedstaden and Region Sjælland in favour of a single national EPJ, citing cost, inefficiency and data-sovereignty concerns; roughly half of candidates in the November 2025 Region Østdanmark council elections favoured replacing the platform, and Prime Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen has separately and repeatedly pushed for Denmark to move to one national EPJ system — a live political risk to Epic’s eastern-Denmark incumbency that FHI will track as a standing watch item.
  • Sixteen sites carry a confirmed EPR data gap: all ten private multi-region chain sites (Aleris, five sites; Capio, five sites), five further independent private hospitals (Roskilde Privathospital, Skovhus Privathospital, Dansk Gigthospital, Privathospitalet Mølholm, AROS Privathospital), and the self-governing Epilepsihospitalet Filadelfia have no publicly documented EPR system and are flagged as unconfirmed rather than guessed.

1. National context: five regions, two vendors, and a reorganisation already under way

Denmark’s public hospital system is organised around five administrative regions — Hovedstaden, Sjælland, Syddanmark, Midtjylland and Nordjylland — each of which owns and runs its own hospitals and, critically, procures its own EPR platform independently. Unlike Ireland’s single national HSE-run tender, or the Netherlands’ hospital-by-hospital vendor-led market, Denmark sits at a third structural point: regional monopoly procurement, where one region’s EPR choice becomes every hospital in that region’s EPR, with no site-level variation found across the 85 sites mapped for this report.

The result is unusually legible for a national market of this size. Two regions — Hovedstaden and Sjælland — jointly run Sundhedsplatformen (“the Health Platform”), an Epic-based EPR that went live in Hovedstaden in 2016 and Sjælland in 2017 [1][2]. The remaining three regions all run Systematic’s Columna CIS platform, but under three separate regional brand names reflecting three separate procurement histories: EPJ SYD in Region Syddanmark, live 2021–2022 after Systematic won a 2018 EU tender in which Sundhedsplatformen/Epic was evaluated and rejected [3][4][5][6]; MidtEPJ in Region Midtjylland, live region-wide since 2013 — one of Denmark’s earliest and most complete single-system EPR rollouts [7]; and NordEPJ in Region Nordjylland, live since March 2022 and described in regional communications as the region’s largest-ever IT project [8].

Sundhedsdatastyrelsen (the Danish Health Data Authority) sits above the five regions as the national body responsible for health data infrastructure and health registers, though day-to-day national health-data exchange runs through the separate MedCom network; neither runs or mandates a specific regional EPR platform [9]. That regional autonomy is precisely what is now under political pressure: the 2024 sundhedsreform (national health reform) is merging Region Hovedstaden and Region Sjælland into a single “Region Østdanmark” effective 1 January 2027 [10], and Prime Minister Løkke Rasmussen has separately and repeatedly stated his ambition for Denmark to consolidate onto one national EPJ system altogether [11] — a much larger structural question than the Hovedstaden/Sjælland merger alone, since it would require unwinding the Epic/Systematic split that currently defines the market.

2. Region Hovedstaden & Region Sjælland: Sundhedsplatformen (Epic)

Hovedstaden and Sjælland are treated together here because they jointly run the same EPR platform and are merging into one region from 2027. Hovedstaden covers Copenhagen, its western suburbs, North Zealand and Bornholm; Sjælland covers the rest of Zealand, Lolland-Falster and the small islands. Together they account for 33 of the 85 sites mapped in this report — 32 confirmed on Sundhedsplatformen and one (Epilepsihospitalet Filadelfia, a self-governing hospital operating under a service contract rather than direct regional control) unconfirmed.

🔍 Supplier Watch — Sundhedsplatformen under sustained political pressure

Sundhedsplatformen is the only Epic deployment in Denmark and covers the entire eastern half of the country’s public hospital system by design — but it is also the most politically contested EPR in the Nordic region. Beyond the 2026 borgerforslag and PM Løkke’s national-EPJ ambition (see Conclusions), the platform has drawn criticism since its 2016–2017 rollout over usability and clinician workflow; FHI will track both the borgerforslag’s outcome and any formal regional procurement activity signalling a platform review as standing watch items.

3. Region Syddanmark: EPJ SYD (Systematic Columna CIS)

Region Syddanmark covers Funen and South/Southwest Jutland and is the one region in this report with a directly documented head-to-head vendor competition: its 2017–2018 EU tender for a region-wide EPJ replacement evaluated Sundhedsplatformen/Epic alongside other bidders and selected Systematic’s Columna CIS platform instead, branded locally as EPJ SYD [4]. Go-live proceeded in three regional waves: Sygehus Sønderjylland (Aabenraa) on 3 October 2021, Odense Universitetshospital on 20 February 2022, and Sydvestjysk Sygehus/Sygehus Lillebælt on 29 May 2022 [5][6]. All 16 sites mapped in this region are confirmed on EPJ SYD.

11. Data gaps, methodology and recommended next steps

This report was built from a single research pass across regional and hospital-group websites, Danske Regioner’s national EPJ fact sheet, Region Syddanmark’s, Region Nordjylland’s and Aalborg Universitetshospital’s own press releases, Systematic’s and Danish trade/general press coverage (Version2, Altinget, sn.dk, TV2 Fyn, HK), Danske Regioner’s national health-reform documentation, and (for this edition) Danmarks Statistik, individual hospital nøgletal pages, Trap Danmark and the official sites of Denmark’s national health-IT governance bodies, gathered 31 August–1 September 2026. It is a strong first layer for the Denmark database, with four structural gaps FHI should close before treating any record as complete:

  • Sixteen sites have no confirmed EPR system. All 15 private hospital sites (Aleris, Capio and five independents) and Epilepsihospitalet Filadelfia are flagged unconfirmed rather than guessed. A direct outreach pass to the two private chains’ head offices, and to Filadelfia’s administration, is the natural way to close this gap.
  • Bed counts are confirmed for 50 of 85 sites (≈59%). The remainder are either published only as a combined group-level figure that cannot be split by site (a recurring pattern at multi-campus groups such as Rigshospitalet, Herlev/Gentofte and SUH), genuinely not published, or a confirmed zero at outpatient-only sites. A direct data request to each region’s capacity-planning office is the most reliable way to close the remaining gap with site-level precision.
  • The Region Østdanmark merger (1 Jan 2027) has not yet produced any documented EPR-consolidation decision. Because Hovedstaden and Sjælland already share Sundhedsplatformen, the merger should be organisationally rather than technically disruptive — but FHI found no formal statement confirming this, and it should be verified directly once the merger is closer to taking effect.
  • Three major construction projects (Nyt Hospital Nordsjælland, Nyt OUH, and the SUH Køge consolidation) are running late with no confirmed IT/medical-equipment integration tender identified for any of the three. These are flagged as future procurement-notice candidates rather than backfilled with an assumed figure.

13. Conclusions

Denmark’s EPR market is, structurally, the simplest of the three Tier 1 countries mapped so far in this database: five regions, two vendors, zero cross-boundary exceptions found. But “simple” is not the same as “stable”, and several live developments this year mean the current map should be read as a snapshot of a system in the early stages of political, organisational and governance change, not a settled equilibrium.

The Sundhedsplatformen question: from regional merger to national-EPJ ambition.

Two distinct but related developments are converging on Epic’s eastern-Denmark incumbency. First, the 2024 sundhedsreform is merging Region Hovedstaden and Region Sjælland into “Region Østdanmark” from 1 January 2027 [10] — a change that should be technically uneventful for the EPR itself, since both regions already run Sundhedsplatformen, but which consolidates governance over the platform’s roughly 33 sites under a single new regional authority for the first time. Second, and more consequentially, an active 2026 borgerforslag is calling for Sundhedsplatformen to be abolished outright in favour of a unified national EPJ, citing cost, inefficiency and data-sovereignty concerns tied to Epic being a US company [20]; roughly half of candidates in the November 2025 Region Østdanmark council elections favoured replacing the platform, and Prime Minister Løkke Rasmussen has separately and repeatedly stated his own ambition for Denmark to move to a single national EPJ system [11].

Neither development is a formal procurement action yet, and the borgerforslag’s signature window closed 1 September 2026 without (per current information) reaching the threshold that would force a parliamentary debate. But the combination — a governance consolidation already locked in for 2027, a politically live abolition campaign, a Prime Minister publicly on record favouring one national system, and a simultaneous restructuring of the national data-governance layer itself (Digital Sundhed Danmark, §12) — is a materially different risk profile from the Netherlands’ UMCG dispute (a single regional contract-extension question) or Ireland’s incremental national EHR rollout. If a national single-EPJ push gains real momentum, it would not just threaten Sundhedsplatformen’s eastern-Denmark position but potentially force a decision on which of the two incumbent platforms — or a third option — becomes the national standard, affecting all 85 sites mapped in this report rather than a subset. FHI will track the borgerforslag’s formal outcome, any national-EPJ policy statement from the Ministry of Health, the Region Østdanmark transition, and the naming of Digital Sundhed Danmark’s permanent board (expected H2 2026) as a standing watch cluster.

Separately, the near-simultaneous completion of three major infrastructure projects — Aalborg Universitetshospital’s new Hospitalsbyen campus (inaugurated May 2026), Region Hovedstaden’s psychiatry reorganisation and Region Sjælland’s MVH merger (both 1 Jan 2026) — shows a health system absorbing a significant amount of organisational change in a very short window, even before the 2027 regional merger takes effect. FHI will track whether the three still-delayed supersygehus builds (Nordsjælland, Nyt OUH, SUH Køge) generate confirmed IT/medical-equipment procurement notices as they approach completion, as a distinct monitoring line from the political EPR-consolidation question above.
