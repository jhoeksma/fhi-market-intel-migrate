Hungary's HIS landscape: the state's live centralisation onto e-MedSolution, tracked hospital by hospital

Hungary gives this database its first genuine national-centralisation story, structurally the opposite of every country mapped so far. Where Austria faces a shared-legacy sunset (SAP IS-H, 2030) and Sweden a failed regional procurement (Oracle Health Millennium), Hungary's state is actively consolidating its entire public hospital estate onto one platform, e-MedSolution -- a hospital information system originally built by T-Systems Magyarorszag, made mandatory for "more than two-thirds" of OKFO's 80 state institutions from January 2025. This report captures that process mid-flight: a full 80-institution OKFO roster (captured directly from OKFO's own site, a Tier 1 primary source), and 24 individually confirmed e-MedSolution relationships against it -- 16 named with exact go-live dates from two official ESZFK rollout-schedule documents (Phase I, February-May 2025; Phase II, August-November 2025), two more named by a trade-press critic as early adopters, two more confirmed independently via primary sources (a 2021 procurement notice, a 2023 hospital IT registry), and 3 of Hungary's 4 medical universities running the platform voluntarily, years before the mandate existed. Ownership of e-MedSolution itself does not trace to a single clean transaction: T-Systems Magyarorszag was sold by Magyar Telekom to 4iG in July 2019, and the state's own operator, ESZFK, is confirmed as e-MedSolution's vendor-of-record only by 2023 -- a multi-year process, not a purchase date. The remaining majority of OKFO's estate -- 59 institutions -- is recorded here as covered by the mandate but individually unconfirmed, an honest reflection of a rollout that is real, dated, and ongoing, not yet complete.

Key findings

  • An active, state-mandated national consolidation -- not a legacy risk or a failed procurement -- is Hungary's headline thread. From January 2025, e-MedSolution became mandatory for "more than two-thirds" of OKFO's 80 state institutions (Telex/HVG, both citing Nepszava, Oct 2024). Universities, church-run, municipal and independent-specialist institutions are formally exempt -- the exact regulatory wording is now directly sourced.
  • Two official ESZFK rollout-schedule PDFs name 16 hospitals with exact go-live dates -- the single most concrete, dated evidence base this database has yet obtained for an in-progress national rollout anywhere. Phase I (published Feb 2025) starts with Ajkai Magyar Imre Korhaz on 1 February 2025 and reaches 7 more by 1 May 2025; Phase II (published Sep 2025) adds 8 more across August, September and November 2025.
  • e-MedSolution's ownership history does not reduce to one clean "state acquisition" date. Earlier secondary reporting suggested a 2022 state purchase from T-Systems; this pass could not corroborate that. Instead, T-Systems Magyarorszag itself was sold by its parent Magyar Telekom to 4iG (led by Jaszai Gellert, previously of Lorinc Meszaros's business empire) in July 2019, and ESZFK is separately confirmed as e-MedSolution's vendor-of-record only by 2023, via a hospital's own IT-systems registry -- a gradual shift in operational control, not a single transaction.
  • 3 of Hungary's 4 medical universities already ran e-MedSolution/its MedSolution predecessor voluntarily, years before any mandate existed. Semmelweis (Budapest), Szeged and Pecs are all confirmed live -- Szeged's own 2017 procurement notice is this report's earliest confirmed e-MedSolution deployment anywhere in Hungary. Only Debrecen runs a genuinely independent, self-developed system (UD MED) -- the same self-build pattern this database has already documented at KAGes (Austria) and IKEM (Czechia).
  • A named critic's pre-mandate count and the official post-mandate rollout schedule tell two different chapters of the same story, not a contradiction. Laszlo Rasky, Secretary-General of the Hungarian Medical Technology Association, named only two hospitals -- Sarvar and Farkasgyepu -- as successfully migrated at the time of his remarks, warning that "400-500 people's livelihoods" were at risk and that a realistic hospital IT changeover needs 3-6 months. Read alongside the later, dated ESZFK schedules, this reads as an early, contested snapshot that the official rollout then built on, not a conflicting data point to resolve away.
  • The remaining 59 OKFO institutions are covered by the mandate but individually unconfirmed -- an honest reflection of an incomplete, in-progress rollout rather than a research gap in the conventional sense. Unlike Krajska zdravotni in Czechia (a stable structure this database failed to resolve after two research passes), most of these 59 will very plausibly show a confirmed e-MedSolution deployment in a follow-up pass, simply because the rollout has not finished yet.

1. National context: a merged interior/health portfolio and the most centralised hospital-ownership model this database has mapped

Hungary has no standalone Ministry of Health. Health policy sits under the Ministry of Interior (Belugyminiszterium) via a State Secretariat for Health, currently led by Dr. Takacs Peter -- a genuinely merged portfolio, not an arm's-length agency relationship, and a materially more unusual structure than any other country this database has mapped. OKFO (Orszagos Korhazi Foigazgatosag, successor to AEEK) exercises state ownership over a confirmed 80 named hospitals/institutions -- captured directly from OKFO's own site this pass, a Tier 1 primary source that supersedes the ~102 figure carried in earlier secondary reporting. This is a materially more centralised hospital-ownership model than any country this database has mapped so far: closer to a single national hospital trust than the federal (Austria) or regional-holding (Czechia, Belgium) patterns already documented.

ESZFK (Egeszsegugyi Informatikai Szolgaltato es Fejlesztesi Kozpont), a state-owned nonprofit, develops and operates the centralised e-MedSolution platform and is the source of the two dated national rollout-schedule PDFs this report draws on directly. DKU (Digitalis Kormanyzati Ugynokseg) runs centralised IT procurement frameworks -- the largest single buyer under CPV 48814000 in this database's Hungary TED sweep. EESZT (live since 1 November 2017) is the national interoperability layer, kept conceptually distinct from e-MedSolution itself, the same "data-exchange backbone vs. HIS product" distinction this database has already drawn for Austria's ELGA and Czechia's UZIS. The 4 medical universities (Semmelweis, Pecs, Szeged, Debrecen) sit outside OKFO's ownership, under the Ministry of Culture and Innovation, and are formally exempt from the e-MedSolution mandate -- though, as §2 shows, most already run the platform anyway.

2. Medical university clinical centres: 3 of 4 run e-MedSolution voluntarily, years before any mandate existed

Semmelweis University (Budapest), Szegedi Tudomanyegyetem (Szent-Gyorgyi Albert Klinikai Kozpont) and Pecsi Tudomanyegyetem Klinikai Kozpont all run e-MedSolution/e-Medsolution voluntarily -- Szeged's own 2017 procurement notice, bundling e-MedSolution/GLIMS support, is this report's earliest confirmed e-MedSolution deployment anywhere in Hungary, predating the January 2025 mandate by nearly eight years. Both Szeged and Pecs also run GLIMS, a bundled laboratory information system, alongside e-MedSolution -- an extended category, not core EPR, noted here for completeness. Debreceni Egyetem Klinikai Kozpont is the sole exception: a genuinely independent, self-developed system, UD MED, confirmed via the university's own portal -- the same self-build pattern this database has already documented at KAGes (Austria's openMEDOCS) and IKEM (Czechia's Zlatokop).

3. The e-MedSolution national rollout: 16 OKFO hospitals with exact, dated go-live confirmations

ESZFK has published two "e-MedSolution orszagos kiterjesztes" (national extension) rollout-schedule documents -- the most concrete, dated primary-source evidence this database has yet obtained for an in-progress national IT rollout anywhere. Phase I (v1.0, published Feb 2025, overall phase deadline 31 December 2025) starts with Ajkai Magyar Imre Korhaz going live on 1 February 2025, followed by Papai Grof Esterhazy Korhaz, Nagyatadi Korhaz, Del-Budai Centrumkorhaz Szent Imre Egyetemi Oktatokorhaz, Karcagi Katai Gabor Korhaz, Mezoturi Korhaz, Oroshazi Dr. Laszlo Elek Korhaz and Jaszberenyi Szent Erzsebet Korhaz, all live by 1 May 2025. Phase II (v2.0, published Sep 2025) adds Nograd Varmegyei Szent Lazar Korhaz, Pasztoi Margit Korhaz, Heves Varmegyei Markhot Ferenc Oktatokorhaz and Hatvani Albert Schweitzer Korhaz on 1 August 2025; Gyongyosi Bugat Pal Korhaz on 1 September 2025; and Komarom-Esztergom Varmegyei Szent Borbala Korhaz, Kisberi Batthyany Kazmer Szakkorhaz and Oroszlanyi Szakorvosi Intezet on 1 November 2025. No Phase III document was found this pass -- either it has not yet been published, or the residual coverage needed to reach "more than two-thirds of institutions" is being documented on a rolling, per-institution basis rather than a further consolidated schedule.

4. Confirmed outside the official schedule: two early adopters, two independent primary-source confirmations, and one affiliated-but-unconfirmed hospital

Sarvari Szent Laszlo Korhaz and Farkasgyepui Tudogyogyintezet were named by Laszlo Rasky, Secretary-General of the Hungarian Medical Technology Association, as the only two institutions successfully migrated to e-MedSol at the time of his remarks -- a pre-mandate snapshot, contemporaneous with the October 2024 centralisation-announcement wave, that the later official Phase I/II schedules then built on. Rasky also warned that "400-500 people's livelihoods" were at risk from the changeover and that a realistic hospital IT transition needs 3-6 months, not the pace a rushed centralisation implies -- a genuine critical voice worth preserving alongside the official timeline, not smoothing over.

Fejer Megyei Szent Gyorgy Egyetemi Oktato Korhaz is independently confirmed running e-MedSolution as far back as March 2021, via the hospital's own maintenance-services procurement notice (2,730 dedicated + 100 concurrent licences, T-Systems Magyarorszag Zrt. the sole bidder for ongoing support) -- years before the mandate, and while T-Systems, not ESZFK, was still the named contracted maintainer. Gottsegen Gyorgy Orszagos Kardiovaszkularis Intezet is confirmed via its own 2023 IT-systems registry, which names ESZFK -- not T-Systems -- as vendor (440 named user licences, 100 database-management licences, 18 communication licences), the clearest single data point this report has for when operational control of e-MedSolution had shifted to the state's own operator. Gottsegen also runs iSort (Neumann Medical Kft.), a cardiac-surgery documentation system two-way integrated with e-MedSolution -- an ancillary specialist system, not core EPR.

Kiskunhalasi Semmelweis Korhaz is named directly on OKFO's own site as "a Szegedi Tudomanyegyetem Oktato Korhaza" (a University of Szeged teaching hospital) -- a Tier-1-confirmed affiliation. Whether it shares Szeged's e-MedSolution/GLIMS platform, however, is an inference rather than an independently confirmed fact, and is recorded here as such.

6. The remaining OKFO county and district hospitals: the bulk of the mandate, not yet individually confirmed

9. Methodology and evidentiary notes

This report combines a first research pass, a same-week deeper-dive pass, and a figures-chase pass (11-12 Sep 2026). First pass established Hungary's unusual national governance structure and identified the e-MedSolution centralisation as the report's headline thread via national trade press and a TED sweep (CPV 48814000: 53 notices; CPV 48180000: 121 notices, browser-routed -- this research environment's own sandbox cannot reach api.ted.europa.eu directly, the same network-egress restriction noted for every prior country in this database). The deeper-dive pass captured the full 80-institution OKFO roster directly from OKFO's own paginated site, and located two Tier-1 ESZFK rollout-schedule PDFs naming 16 hospitals with exact go-live dates -- resolving what pass 1 flagged as "the single most valuable confirmable fact this report can add." It also revised pass 1's "2022 state acquisition" claim, which could not be corroborated, in favour of a better-evidenced two-step ownership history (T-Systems Magyarorszag's 2019 sale to 4iG; ESZFK's 2023 confirmation as vendor-of-record). The figures-chase pass sourced the EUR/HUF rate, confirmed the HUF currency of the "~2bn" annual market-value figure, found two more individually confirmed hospitals (Fejer, Gottsegen) via primary IT-registry/procurement documents, and identified a named national digital-leadership contact.

Deployment-row philosophy follows this database's standard approach: a deployment row was created only where genuine cited evidence exists for that specific institution. The 59 OKFO institutions not individually confirmed this pass (§5-6) deliberately carry no deployment row at all rather than an assumed or guessed e-MedSolution entry -- consistent with this database's treatment of Stredocesky kraj's unconsolidated hospitals in Czechia. Kiskunhalasi Semmelweis Korhaz's e-MedSolution relationship is recorded with status="unconfirmed" despite its Tier-1-confirmed Szeged affiliation, since platform-sharing itself is an inference.

Bed counts are not systematically sourced this pass (shown as n/k throughout). NEAK (the National Health Insurance Fund) publishes an official hospital bed-count and patient-flow report, but the direct file link returned a 404 and the listing directory did not resolve via fetch (likely JS-rendered) -- flagged as a follow-up rather than repeatedly reattempted this pass, consistent with the partial bed-count coverage this database has accepted for other countries' first passes.

The 45 county/district OKFO institutions in §6 and 14 Budapest institutions in §5 are this report's largest open item, but differ structurally from a genuine research gap like Krajska zdravotni in Czechia: the underlying fact (whether they run e-MedSolution) is not stably unknown, it is actively changing as the mandate's rollout continues. A follow-up pass timed after further ESZFK phase publications, or a direct enquiry to OKFO/ESZFK, is the recommended next step rather than a repeat generic web sweep.

10. National health-IT governance: bodies and current leadership

Hungarian hospital-IT-relevant governance sits almost entirely at the national level: a merged Interior/Health ministry portfolio, OKFO's direct ownership of 80 state hospitals, ESZFK's operational control of the mandated national HIS platform, and DKU's centralised procurement role. No regional or county-level hospital-ownership layer exists comparable to Austria's Lander or Czechia's kraje -- Hungary's structure is the most centralised this database has yet mapped. The table below sets out the bodies FHI will track as the national layer of the Hungarian market.

Belugyminiszterium

Ministry of Interior

Hungary has no standalone Ministry of Health -- health policy sits under the Ministry of Interior via a State Secretariat for Health, currently led by Dr. Takacs Peter (Egeszsegugyert Felelos Allamtitkar). A materially more unusual governance structure than any other country this database has mapped: most peer countries run health through a dedicated ministry or an arm's-length agency, not a merged interior/health portfolio.

OKFO

Orszagos Korhazi Foigazgatosag (National Directorate General for Hospitals)

Exercises state ownership over a confirmed 80 named hospitals/institutions -- "human resource and capacity management, quality assurance, continuous improvement of operational efficiency, centralised public procurement" per its own site. Successor to AEEK. IT leadership: Miklovicz Zoltan, Informatikai foigazgato-helyettes (Deputy Director-General for IT) since 1 April 2023, previously Competence Center director at T-Systems Hungary (2016-2020) -- a direct personal link into the e-MedSolution vendor lineage.

ESZFK

Egeszsegugyi Informatikai Szolgaltato es Fejlesztesi Kozpont

State-owned nonprofit company under the Interior Ministry; develops/operates the centralised e-MedSolution platform and publishes its national rollout schedule (the two "orszagos kiterjesztes" Phase I/II PDFs this report draws on directly). CEO: Tolnay Roland.

DKU

Digitalis Kormanyzati Ugynokseg (Digital Government Agency)

Runs centralised IT procurement frameworks/DPS for the Hungarian state -- the largest single buyer under CPV 48814000 in this database's Hungary TED sweep (14 of 53 notices, 2021-2022), the plausible procurement vehicle around the state's move to e-MedSolution vendor control.

EESZT

Elektronikus Egeszsegugyi Szolgaltatasi Ter

Hungary's national interoperability/data-exchange layer, live since 1 November 2017 -- Hungary's analogue to Austria's ELGA, Czechia's UZIS, or Denmark's MedCom. Kept conceptually distinct from e-MedSolution: EESZT is the backbone every system connects to, not itself a HIS product.

Medical universities

The 4 medical universities (Semmelweis, Pecs, Szeged, Debrecen) run their own clinical centres largely independent of OKFO, under the Ministry of Culture and Innovation. Nominally exempt from the e-MedSolution mandate, but 3 of 4 already run it voluntarily.

Universities, church-run, municipal and independent-specialist institutions are all formally exempt from the mandate -- per Telex's direct quote of the regulation: "Az egyetemek, egyhazi es onkormanyzati intezmenyek es az onallo szakrendelok."

Sourced from each organisation's own official pages (okfo.gov.hu, eszfk.hu, e-egeszsegugy.gov.hu) plus named trade press (Telex, HVG, eGov Hirlevel, Economx) for the centralisation narrative, accessed 11-12 September 2026.

11. Conclusions

Hungary is this database's first genuine national-centralisation story: a state actively consolidating its entire public hospital IT estate onto one platform under a hard, dated mandate, rather than facing a shared legacy risk (Austria) or a failed regional procurement (Sweden). The evidence base this report assembled -- an 80-institution primary-source roster, two dated official rollout schedules naming 16 hospitals, and independent primary-source confirmations for 4 more -- gives FHI a genuinely rare thing in this market: a live, trackable, government-published implementation timetable rather than a static snapshot. For FHI's commercial purposes, the practical implication is that this report's confirmed-vendor count will grow simply with time and further ESZFK phase publications, unlike markets where an unconfirmed vendor reflects a stable information gap. e-MedSolution's ownership history -- a 2019 change of control at T-Systems Magyarorszag followed by a gradual shift of operational control to the state's own ESZFK by 2023 -- is also a distinctive pattern worth tracking across this database: state consolidation achieved through supplier-ownership drift and operational takeover, rather than a clean asset purchase.

Watch item: a Phase III ESZFK rollout-schedule document, if and when published, would be this report's single highest-value update -- it would very plausibly convert a large share of the 59 currently-unconfirmed OKFO institutions into confirmed e-MedSolution deployments in one step.
Watch item: Kiskunhalasi Semmelweis Korhaz's exact platform-sharing status with Szeged is a concrete, narrow, single-institution confirmation that should be resolvable in a follow-up pass -- a good quick win.
Watch item: the "~2 billion HUF" annual e-MedSolution market-value figure is reported at a level of precision ("approximately") that has not improved since pass 1 -- worth a dedicated trade-press search (IME/ITBusiness market-sizing coverage) in any future pass, or dropping the figure if it cannot be sharpened.
Watch item: NEAK's public hospital bed-count file ("Korhazi agyszam- es betegforgalmi kimutatas 2025") could not be retrieved this pass despite being publicly referenced -- worth a direct retry or a request routed through NEAK's contact channel, since it would resolve this report's only remaining systematic data gap in one document.
