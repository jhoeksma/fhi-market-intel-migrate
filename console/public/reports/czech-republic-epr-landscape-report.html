Czechia's EPR/NIS landscape: a domestic STAPRO/ICZ duopoly, AGEL's self-built IKIS, and Europe's most fragmented procurement pattern yet mapped

The Czech Republic gives this database its largest and most fragmented hospital-IT market to date: 35 hospital groups and 80 sites, built from two research passes (a first pass and a same-day deeper-dive uplift, mirroring the Austria and Belgium methodology) that between them resolved core-NIS vendor confirmations for 5 of 7 major regional/private operators and 11 of 12 university hospitals. The headline structural finding is a domestic two-vendor duopoly: STAPRO s.r.o. (FONS Enterprise, the modern line; Medea, an older but still-live line) and ICZ a.s. (AMIS*HD modern, AMIS*H legacy) between them cover most of the confirmed regional-holding and university-hospital estate, including a genuine domestic-vendor success story at Zdravotnicky holding Kralovehradeckeho kraje -- described by ICZ itself as "the first unified hospital-IS solution of this type implemented by a region in the Czech Republic" when signed in 2019. AGEL a.s., Czechia's largest private hospital group, runs a wholly self-developed and owned platform (IKIS, via subsidiary Medical Systems) across all 12 of its Czech sites -- and, notably, also licenses it to FN Ostrava, a state-owned teaching hospital outside the AGEL group entirely. Set against this confirmed structure sits a genuinely unresolved gap at Krajska zdravotni, a.s. (5 hospitals, Usti nad Labem region), which stayed open despite a dedicated second-pass research effort -- this report's equivalent of the OOe Gesundheitsholding gap this database first documented in Austria -- and an even larger fragmentation signal: a TED sweep on CPV 48814000 for buyer-country=CZE returned 843 notices, the largest single-CPV result of any country this database has swept, confirming that most Czech hospitals procure IT one institution at a time rather than through group-wide contracts.

Key findings

  • A genuine domestic two-vendor duopoly covers most of the confirmed public estate. STAPRO s.r.o. (FONS Enterprise/Medea) and ICZ a.s. (AMIS*HD/AMIS*H) together account for the vast majority of this report's confirmed group-wide and university-hospital deployments -- Zdravotnicky holding Kralovehradeckeho kraje (ICZ, 4 sites), Nemocnice Pardubickeho kraje (STAPRO, 5 sites) and Kraj Vysocina (STAPRO, 5 sites) each confirmed as a unified group-wide platform, alongside individual confirmations at 8 of Czechia's 12 fakultni nemocnice (teaching hospitals). Unlike most other markets this database has mapped, the dominant vendors here are domestic Czech companies, not international commercial EHR groups.
  • AGEL a.s. runs Czechia's largest confirmed self-developed platform, and licenses it outside its own group. IKIS, built and owned by AGEL's wholly-owned subsidiary Medical Systems, is confirmed across all 12 of AGEL's Czech hospitals -- and, notably, is also the confirmed system at Fakultni nemocnice Ostrava, a state-owned teaching hospital with no AGEL ownership stake, evidence that IKIS has become a genuine independent product in the Czech market rather than a purely in-house tool.
  • Krajska zdravotni, a.s. remains genuinely unresolved after a dedicated deeper-dive research pass -- national vendor-name searches, the group's own 2021/2022 annual reports (which name digital leadership, Jan Pejchal, but no product), a TED buyer-name query restricted to CPV 48814000/48180000, and Czech public-contracts sources were all checked. The only signal found was a small 2018 award (CZK 2.98m (≈€122.9k)) to a local Usti nad Labem reseller, almost certainly a module rather than the core platform. This is recorded here in the same spirit as this database's other honestly-open gaps (Austria's OOe Gesundheitsholding) rather than guessed at.
  • FN Motol a Homolka, Czechia's largest hospital, is live on InterSystems IRIS for Health per a 7 August 2026 TED licence-expansion notice -- superseding or coexisting with its 1998-vintage UNIS Steiner legacy platform, itself still recorded here pending clarification of exactly how the two systems currently divide clinical scope.
  • Procurement is genuinely the most fragmented this database has yet swept: 843 TED notices for CPV 48814000 alone (more than double Austria's 100 and far beyond any Nordic country's group-consolidated pattern), plus a further 391 for the broader CPV 48180000 code, surfacing a long tail of standalone specialist and regional hospitals (Nemocnice Na Homolce, CKTCH Brno, Masarykuv onkologicky ustav, Narodni ustav dusevniho zdravi and others) each independently procuring IT with no core vendor confirmed for any of them yet.
  • Penta Hospitals CZ's 15-site portfolio was reconciled this pass but its vendor remains unconfirmed -- 2025/2026 press coverage of the "Group Mediterra" acquisition (Melnik, Mostiste, Tanvald, Sedlcany hospitals plus the Malvazinky rehabilitation clinic and CLPA orthopaedics/musculoskeletal clinic) confirmed the portfolio already researched is current, not stale, but no source reviewed names Penta's core clinical IT system.

1. National context: ministry-run teaching hospitals, mixed regional consolidation, and a domestic vendor duopoly

Czech hospital governance splits three ways. The Ministry of Health (MZCR) directly owns and manages most fakultni nemocnice (teaching hospitals) and national specialised centres -- a materially more hands-on role than the arm's-length funding-body pattern this database has documented in most other countries. The 14 kraje (regions) own and operate regional hospitals, but consolidation into a single group-wide entity is genuinely inconsistent: Kralovehradecky, Pardubicky, Vysocina and Usti nad Labem kraje each run a unified hospital holding, while Stredocesky kraj's regional hospitals remain five entirely separate single-site joint-stock companies, and several other kraje have not consolidated their hospital estate into any single operating entity at all. UZIS CR (Ustav zdravotnickych informaci a statistiky CR) runs the national eHealth/statistics layer -- Czechia's analogue to Austria's ELGA GmbH or Denmark's Sundhedsdatastyrelsen -- but, as in every other country this database has mapped, does not standardise which NIS a given hospital group runs.

Against that mixed governance backdrop, the vendor landscape itself is unusually concentrated for a market this fragmented at the procurement level: STAPRO s.r.o. and ICZ a.s., both Czech domestic software companies, between them supply most of this report's confirmed group-wide and university-hospital deployments. STAPRO's modern FONS Enterprise line covers Nemocnice Pardubickeho kraje and Kraj Vysocina group-wide plus Fakultni Thomayerova nemocnice individually, while its older Medea line remains live at Vseobecna fakultni nemocnice (since 1996, modernised rather than replaced) and Fakultni nemocnice Olomouc (since 2005, now under active multi-phase replacement tender). ICZ's modern AMIS*HD covers Zdravotnicky holding Kralovehradeckeho kraje group-wide and, per the company's own case study, Fakultni nemocnice Brno's "most extensive hospital information system implementation in the Czech Republic" (1,000+ end workstations); its older AMIS*H remains live at Ustredni vojenska nemocnice Praha since 1993, now under active replacement tender. A TED CPV 48814000 sweep for buyer-country=CZE returned 843 notices -- far larger than any prior country this database has swept -- confirming that beyond these confirmed group-wide platforms, most of the remaining Czech hospital estate procures IT one institution at a time.

5. AGEL a.s.: Czechia's largest private group, self-developed IKIS across all 12 sites, licensed outside the group too

AGEL a.s. is Czechia's largest private hospital group, with ~13,000 staff across Czech and Slovak operations and 12 confirmed hospital sites spread across 6 different kraje (Moravskoslezsky, Olomoucky, Zlinsky, Ustecky and Stredocesky), reflecting AGEL's acquisition-led growth rather than any single regional footprint. The group runs IKIS (Integrovany klinicky informacni system), developed and owned by its wholly-owned subsidiary Medical Systems, confirmed group-wide across all 12 sites via the company's own corporate pages. 6. Krajska zdravotni, a.s.: genuinely unresolved after a dedicated deeper-dive pass

Krajska zdravotni, a.s. runs 5 hospitals in the Usti nad Labem region (Masarykova nemocnice Usti nad Labem, Most, Decin, Chomutov, Teplice) with no core NIS vendor confirmed despite a dedicated second-pass research effort. 10. Prague's fakultni nemocnice: a mix of legacy domestic platforms and a confirmed InterSystems deployment

Prague hosts 7 of this report's confirmed teaching/state hospitals, each governed directly by MZCR rather than through a kraj structure. FN v Motole, Czechia's largest fakultni nemocnice, ran legacy UNIS Steiner from 1998 and is now confirmed live on InterSystems IRIS for Health per a 7 August 2026 TED licence-expansion notice -- both are recorded here pending clarification of exactly how current clinical scope divides between the two. FN Bulovka runs a self-developed legacy UNIS system from the 1990s, with only the first 4 clinics migrated off it as of the Mar 2024 trade-press survey used for this report -- a replacement vendor was not identified. Ustredni vojenska nemocnice Praha (a military teaching hospital) runs 1993-vintage ICZ AMIS*H, now under active replacement tender (published 8 June 2026). FN Kralovske Vinohrady runs Steiner since 1992, replacement in planning. VFN Praha runs STAPRO Medea since 1996, modernised rather than replaced. 15. Methodology and evidentiary notes

This report combines a first-pass build with a same-day deeper-dive pass (both 10 Sep 2026), following the pattern established for Austria and Belgium. First pass combined national-structure web research with a TED Search API sweep on CPV 48814000 ("medical information systems") for buyer-country=CZE (843 notices, browser-routed -- this research environment's own sandbox cannot reach api.ted.europa.eu directly, a network-egress restriction specific to the sandbox, not to TED). The deeper-dive pass added a second, broader TED sweep (CPV 48180000, "Medical software package", 391 notices) plus targeted primary-source research -- resolving Kraj Vysocina (via Nemocnice Jihlava's own 2023 annual report) and FN Brno (via ICZ's own case study) -- while confirming, after genuine dedicated effort, that Krajska zdravotni and Penta Hospitals CZ remain open.

Deployment-row philosophy follows this database's standard approach: a deployment row was created only where genuine cited evidence exists. For the four hospital groups confirmed to run one vendor across every site (ZHKHK, Nemocnice Pardubickeho kraje, AGEL, Kraj Vysocina), one deployment row was created per site rather than a single group-level row, since this report's table renderer resolves vendor status at site level (the dep_group_wide() bulk-payload helper, established during the Austria pass, is the standard pattern for this structure). FN Motol's two deployment rows (legacy UNIS Steiner and confirmed InterSystems IRIS for Health) are both recorded because public sources this pass did not establish how current clinical scope divides between them. FN Brno's ICZ deployment is deliberately recorded at Tier 6 (a supplier's own case study) with coverage_status="assumed" rather than "confirmed", reflecting that the exact product line is inferred rather than stated. The Krajska zdravotni deployment (North Med) is recorded with status="unconfirmed" precisely because it evidences a module, not the core platform.

Bed counts are almost entirely unconfirmed this pass (shown as n/k throughout) except where a primary source gave a figure directly -- Nemocnice Jihlava's own 2023 annual report is this report's sole exception (~750 beds) -- an explicit gap, flagged consistent with the pattern already seen in Austria and Sweden's first passes, and a clear quick win for any further Czech Republic research.

Krajska zdravotni, a.s. and Penta Hospitals CZ remain the two priority open items despite dedicated deeper-dive research effort on both. For Krajska zdravotni, every generic search avenue available in this environment (vendor-name web search, annual reports, a TED buyer-name query, public-contracts sources) has now been tried without result -- a direct-observation approach (job postings, LinkedIn, or supplier-side confirmation) is the recommended next step rather than repeating the same pattern. For Penta, the group's 15-site portfolio is now confirmed current via 2025/2026 acquisition press coverage, but no source names its core clinical system at all -- a genuine information gap rather than a conflicting-signal one. The 9 standalone specialist hospitals surfaced by the CPV 48180000 sweep (§12) and Stredocesky kraj's 5 fragmented hospitals (§9) are recorded as open vendor questions at a lower research-priority tier than these two, since none showed even an indirect vendor signal this pass.

16. National health-IT governance: bodies and current leadership

Czech hospital-IT-relevant governance splits between a ministry with unusually direct hospital-ownership responsibility (MZCR), a national statistics/eHealth body with an active systems-operator role (UZIS CR), and 14 kraje whose approach to regional hospital-IT consolidation ranges from fully unified (4 kraje) to entirely fragmented (Stredocesky and others). No single body plays the vendor-standardising role a Kanta (Finland) or MedCom (Denmark) plays; each hospital group or kraj makes its own NIS decision, the same pattern this database has now documented in Belgium and Austria. The table below sets out the bodies FHI will track as the national layer of the Czech Republic market.

MZCR

Ministerstvo zdravotnictvi Ceske republiky (Ministry of Health)

Central stewardship body: sets national health policy and directly owns/manages most fakultni nemocnice (teaching hospitals) and national specialised centres -- a materially larger direct-ownership role than most peer ministries in this database, which typically fund via regional or insurance intermediaries rather than run hospitals themselves.

UZIS CR

Ustav zdravotnickych informaci a statistiky CR

Runs the National Health Information System (NHIS) and national health registries -- Czechia's analogue to Austria's ELGA GmbH or Denmark's Sundhedsdatastyrelsen. Itself an active TED buyer for medical information systems, confirming a direct operational role in national systems rather than a standards-only mandate.

14 kraje (regions)

Own/operate regional hospitals via arm's-length joint-stock companies (a.s.) or, in some regions, unified hospital "holdings" consolidating several sites under one legal entity and IT platform (Kralovehradecky, Pardubicky, Vysocina, Usti nad Labem) -- but this consolidation is not universal: Stredocesky kraj's regional hospitals remain five separate single-site a.s. companies with no group-wide platform, and several kraje (Plzensky, Karlovarsky, Zlinsky) simply have not consolidated their hospital IT at all.

Unlike Austria's clean one-Land-one-operator structure, Czech regional consolidation is genuinely mixed -- readers should not assume every kraj runs (or is moving toward) a single regional hospital-IT platform.

7 public health insurance funds

Payers/purchasers; largest is VZP (Vseobecna zdravotni pojistovna), covering 56% of the population. Insurance financing sits separately from hospital-IT procurement, which remains a hospital-group or kraj-level decision.

Sourced from each organisation's own official pages (uzis.cz) plus general reference sources (World Health Systems Facts, Wikipedia) for national structure, accessed 10 September 2026. Named individual leadership at MZCR and UZIS CR was not independently confirmed this pass -- a follow-up item.

17. Conclusions

The Czech Republic gives this database its largest single-pass hospital-IT market so far -- 35 groups, 80 sites -- and a structural picture unlike any prior country: a genuine domestic two-vendor duopoly (STAPRO and ICZ) covering most of the confirmed regional and university-hospital estate, sitting alongside the most fragmented procurement pattern this database has yet measured (843 TED notices on a single CPV code). AGEL's self-developed IKIS, now licensed outside AGEL's own group to a state-owned teaching hospital, is this report's clearest example of an in-house platform maturing into an independent commercial product. Two priority gaps remain genuinely open after dedicated research effort rather than a lack of trying: Krajska zdravotni's core NIS vendor, and Penta Hospitals CZ's, across its full private-portfolio. For FHI's commercial purposes, the scale of Czechia's fragmented procurement activity -- 391 further notices on the broader CPV 48180000 code alone -- makes this one of the highest-volume near-term opportunity markets in this database to date, even where individual contract values are smaller than the group-wide awards seen in cleaner single-operator markets like Austria or Denmark.

Watch item: Krajska zdravotni's core NIS vendor is this report's single highest-priority open item -- a 5-hospital regional holding with genuinely no public-source signal after two dedicated research passes. A direct-observation approach (job postings, LinkedIn, supplier-side confirmation) rather than a further generic web/TED sweep is the recommended next step.
Watch item: Nemocnice Plzenskeho kraje's live, unawarded shared NIS/ESB procurement -- re-issued at least twice through Sep 2026 -- is a concrete, trackable near-term award to watch; whichever vendor wins would instantly become one of this report's larger confirmed group-wide platforms.
Watch item: FN Olomouc's multi-phase "eHealth a interoperabilita" replacement programme, tendering since May 2022 and still active through Aug 2026, is worth checking for an award in any follow-up pass -- one of this report's longest-running live procurements.
Watch item: FN Brno's ICZ confirmation rests on a single supplier case study (Tier 6) -- corroborating it with an independent source (trade press, the hospital's own site, or a TED notice) is a priority quick-win for any follow-up pass, given the finding's headline weight ("most extensive HIS implementation in the Czech Republic").
Watch item: the 9 standalone specialist hospitals surfaced by the CPV 48180000 sweep (§12) generated real procurement activity (up to 11 notices for a single hospital) with zero vendor confirmations -- a per-institution web-research pass, rather than a further TED sweep, is the recommended approach for this cohort in any future Czech Republic research session.
