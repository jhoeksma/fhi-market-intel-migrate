Austria's EPR/KIS landscape: eight of nine Laender confirmed, CompuGroup Medical's quiet three-way footprint, and a 100-hospital SAP deadline hanging over all of them

Austria's hospital system is organised, unusually cleanly among the countries this database has mapped so far, along its 9 Bundeslaender: each Land runs exactly one public hospital operator, and a coverage-uplift research pass on 10 September 2026 -- the same day as this report's first-pass build -- resolved 8 of those 9 operators to a confirmed core-KIS vendor, up from 4 at first pass. The uplift pass's biggest surprise was not a new vendor but a familiar one in disguise: CompuGroup Medical, already confirmed in Niederoesterreich as "CGM CLINICAL"/NOEKIS, turns out to also supply Vorarlberg's entire public network (as "CGM Clinical", formerly CGM MPA) and Vienna's Wiener Gesundheitsverbund (as "impuls.kis", a legacy-branded product from the same corporate lineage) -- three separately-branded products, one parent company, and Austria's single largest confirmed KIS footprint by site count. That finding sits alongside, not instead of, this report's still-dominant headline risk: trade-press coverage puts more than 100 Austrian hospitals on SAP's IS-H hospital module, which SAP is discontinuing support for by 2030, with roughly half of surveyed hospitals in 2023 doubting they could complete a transition in time -- and the uplift pass added AUVA's national trauma-hospital network (confirmed on Meierhofer's M-KIS, replacing an AUVA legacy in-house system) and a tentative SAP link at Gesundheit Burgenland to the picture. Two further concrete watch items carry over from first pass: Niederoesterreich's EUR 100m+ CGM CLINICAL contract ("NOEKIS", awarded 2019) is running about four years behind its original go-live target, and Vienna's flagship AKH hospital has its own Rechnungshof-audited history of a 65% hospital-IT budget overrun, now confirmed to sit atop a SAP administrative backbone.

Key findings

  • The single biggest fact in this report is still a shared vulnerability, not any one vendor's footprint: per gesundheitswirtschaft.at (2023), almost all public Austrian hospitals -- over 100 institutions -- run SAP's IS-H module, and SAP has announced IS-H end-of-support by 2030. A 2023 industry survey found 49% of Austrian hospitals doubted they could complete a transition in time. Vinzenz Gruppe and Barmherzige Brueder Oesterreich are named as recent IS-H implementers now facing the same deadline, and this coverage-uplift pass adds a tentative SAP link at Gesundheit Burgenland (via a business-intelligence case study, not a direct KIS confirmation) and a confirmed SAP administrative backbone at AKH Wien -- meaning the risk now touches public, private and academic institutions alike.
  • New this pass: CompuGroup Medical is Austria's single most widely-deployed KIS vendor by confirmed site count (37 of 116 sites, across 3 Laender). Niederoesterreich's CGM CLINICAL/"NOEKIS" (25 sites, EUR 100m+/10-year contract, awarded May 2019) was already confirmed at first pass; the uplift pass found Vorarlberg's KHBG running the same corporate family's product under the "CGM Clinical" brand (formerly CGM MPA, rolled out 2016-2018 across all 5 KHBG hospitals) and, most surprisingly, Vienna's Wiener Gesundheitsverbund running "impuls.kis" since 2010-2013 -- built by Systema Human Information Systems GmbH, a CompuGroup subsidiary since 2008 that was formally rebranded CGM Clinical Oesterreich GmbH on 1 Jan 2016, and still named directly in the city's own 2025 digital-services report.
  • AUVA's national trauma-hospital network, unconfirmed at first pass, is now confirmed on Meierhofer's M-KIS -- replacing AUVA's legacy in-house system in a phased rollout with named go-live dates (pilot UKH Salzburg, Nov 2021; UKH Linz, Mar 2022; UKH Klagenfurt planned 2022), reaching an estimated 3,600 users network-wide at full deployment.
  • Niederoesterreich's NOe Landesgesundheitsagentur signed one of the largest single hospital-IT contracts in this database's coverage so far -- CompuGroup Medical's CGM CLINICAL, EUR 100m+ over 10 years, awarded May 2019 across ~19 hospitals/27 locations -- but rollout has run roughly four years behind the original Q1 2021 first-go-live target; only the pilot site, Universitaetsklinikum Neunkirchen, is confirmed live, from end of June 2025.
  • Tirol Kliniken (Oracle Health Millennium, 20+ years), SALK (ORBIS, a long-running and continuously-extended estate) and now KABEG (also ORBIS, confirmed this pass via a 2020 staff-training presentation on its "#orbislernen" rollout) are this report's cleanest confirmed group-wide deployments, each backed by own-site, TED-procurement or detailed secondary-source evidence rather than trade press headlines alone.
  • Only one major public group remains wholly unconfirmed after this coverage-uplift pass: OOe Gesundheitsholding (Oberoesterreich's largest hospital operator, 53.7% market share). A dedicated research effort this pass confirmed only a clinical point-solution (Mint Medical's radiology tumour-diagnostics software at Kepler Universitaetsklinikum) rather than the core KIS -- OOeG is now the clear priority target for any further Austria research, alongside most of the 17 independent Ordensspitaeler and bed-count data, both still `unconfirmed` throughout rather than guessed.

1. National context: a Land-by-Land operating structure, and a shared SAP deadline the structure does not shield against

Austrian hospital financing runs on a two-layer structure familiar from this database's other federal countries: the federal government (via BMSGPK and the national health-insurance framework) funds hospitals' day-to-day operation through 9 Landesgesundheitsfonds, one per Bundesland, on a performance-based (LKF) system, while each Land separately owns and runs its own hospital-operator company -- KAGes in Steiermark, OOe Gesundheitsholding in Oberoesterreich, KABEG in Kaernten, NOe Landesgesundheitsagentur in Niederoesterreich, SALK in Salzburg, Tirol Kliniken in Tirol, KHBG in Vorarlberg, Gesundheit Burgenland, and the Wiener Gesundheitsverbund in Wien. ELGA GmbH, jointly owned by the federal government, all 9 Laender and the social-insurance umbrella body, is the national interoperability layer every hospital's system connects into -- but, exactly as this database found in Belgium, ELGA's mandate is cross-system data exchange, not standardising which KIS/EPR a given hospital operator runs. The result is a market where KIS choice really does track one-vendor-per-Land, a materially cleaner picture than Belgium's hospital-by-hospital fragmentation -- and a coverage-uplift research pass run the same day as this report's first-pass build (10 Sep 2026) resolved 8 of the 9 Land operators to a confirmed vendor, revealing that CompuGroup Medical alone -- under three different product brands -- now accounts for Niederoesterreich, Vorarlberg and Wien.

That clean Land-level picture is overshadowed by a single national-scale risk. Per gesundheitswirtschaft.at's 2023 trade-press coverage ("SAP: Und tschuess!"), almost all public Austrian hospitals -- over 100 institutions -- run SAP's IS-H hospital module, and SAP has confirmed it will discontinue IS-H support by 2030. A contemporaneous survey found 49% of Austrian hospitals doubted they could complete a migration in time; T-Systems Austria and ATSP (Innsbruck) are exploring whether IS-H can be adapted onto SAP's newer S/4HANA architecture ("S4/health") to avoid a full redevelopment, coordinated via SAGA, the Austrian SAP healthcare user group. This is Austria's equivalent of Sweden's Oracle Health Millennium collapse or Denmark's Sundhedsplatformen controversy in narrative weight -- except here the exposure is spread across the majority of the public estate plus, per the same article, Vinzenz Gruppe and Barmherzige Brueder Oesterreich's religious-order hospitals too (see §12), rather than concentrated in one region's failed procurement.

2. Steiermark: KAGes and openMEDOCS, a self-developed KIS with an unreconciled SAP thread

KAGes (Steiermaerkische Krankenanstaltengesellschaft) runs Steiermark's entire public hospital estate across 7 LKH networks and 18 mapped locations, from the flagship LKH-Universitaetsklinikum Graz down to single-site rural LKHs. KAGes runs openMEDOCS, a KIS the company developed itself -- audited as far back as 2004 by the Landesrechnungshof Steiermark (the regional Court of Audit) -- and this remains, per that audit trail, the confirmed base system group-wide. A separate T-Systems Austria case study describes a KAGes "Umstellung des Krankenhausinformationssystems" (KIS conversion) onto a SAP-based platform, executed as a single-night cutover with an ongoing T-Systems hosting partnership -- but public sources available this pass do not reconcile whether this SAP component has superseded openMEDOCS, runs alongside it (e.g. as the ERP/patient-administration layer under an openMEDOCS clinical-documentation front end, a common split elsewhere), or represents a different system entirely. Continuous 2021-2026 TED activity (software-package/information-systems notices, a dedicated laboratory information system in 2024, an operating-theatre integration module at LKH Univ. Klinikum Graz in 2026) confirms ongoing modular extension rather than a wholesale re-platforming. This is the first item to resolve in any Austria coverage-uplift pass: if KAGes turns out to be effectively an IS-H site under the openMEDOCS brand, it would materially enlarge this report's SAP-2030 exposure count.

3. Oberoesterreich: OOe Gesundheitsholding and Klinikum Wels-Grieskirchen, both unconfirmed despite heavy procurement activity

OOe Gesundheitsholding (OOeG, formerly gespag) is Oberoesterreich's largest hospital operator by market share (OOeG's own figure: 53.7%), running 9 mapped sites from the flagship Kepler Universitaetsklinikum in Linz down to smaller regional Klinikum sites. Despite very active 2019-2026 TED procurement history -- a nursing information system (2021), operating-theatre devices, ophthalmology equipment, and a cluster of 2024-2026 RFIs (patient-communication/nursing-task software, automated ICD-10 coding, central bed management, vital-signs capture) -- no core hospital-wide KIS vendor could be confirmed for OOeG this pass. A 2017 joint clinical-information-system tender spanning OOeG and three independent Ordensspitaeler (Klinikum Wels-Grieskirchen, Kreuzschwestern Sierning, and Krankenhaus St. Josef Braunau) was referred to the Landesverwaltungsgericht Oberoesterreich (the regional administrative court) -- evidence of a genuine procurement dispute, but its outcome and any resulting vendor were not established from public sources this pass. Klinikum Wels-Grieskirchen itself -- owned by the Kongregation der Barmherzigen Schwestern vom heiligen Kreuz and the Franziskanerinnen von Voecklabruck (19%), an Ordensspital rather than part of the public OOeG group despite sitting in the same Land -- remains similarly unconfirmed on its current KIS vendor.

5. Niederoesterreich: NOe Landesgesundheitsagentur and NOEKIS -- CGM CLINICAL confirmed group-wide, four years behind schedule

NOe Landesgesundheitsagentur (renamed from the NOe Landeskliniken-Holding on 1 July 2020) runs the largest single hospital group in this report by site count -- 25 mapped Landeskliniken, from 3 university-affiliated clinics (St. Poelten, Krems, Tulln) down to small rural Landesklinika. In May 2019 the group awarded CompuGroup Medical a contract worth over EUR 100 million across 10 years for CGM CLINICAL, branded internally as "NOEKIS" -- at the time covering ~19 hospitals/7,600+ beds/27 locations, and reported as the largest hospital-IT undertaking of its kind in Austria. Rollout has run well behind the original Q1 2021 first-go-live target: per the NOe LGA's own November 2024 press release, only the pilot site, Universitaetsklinikum Neunkirchen, had a confirmed go-live, at the end of June 2025, with a three-phase rollout and full digitisation of patient records targeted for Q1 2026 -- roughly a six-year gap between contract signature and the network's first live site. Every Landesklinikum in this table is recorded here as CGM CLINICAL's confirmed contracted vendor per the group-wide award; individual sites' actual go-live status beyond Neunkirchen is a live, unresolved follow-up item, not yet independently confirmed site-by-site.

15. Methodology and evidentiary notes

This report combines a first-pass build (10 Sep 2026 morning) with a same-day coverage-uplift pass (10 Sep 2026 afternoon), following the pattern established for Belgium. First pass combined national-structure web research with a TED Search API sweep on CPV 48814000 ("medical information systems") for buyer-country=AUT (100 notices, browser-routed -- this research environment's own sandbox cannot reach api.ted.europa.eu directly, a network-egress restriction specific to the sandbox, not to TED). The uplift pass added a second, broader TED sweep (CPV 48180000, "Medical software package", 154 notices) plus targeted web research per remaining unconfirmed group -- resolving 6 of the 7 groups flagged unconfirmed at first pass (KABEG, KHBG, Gesundheit Burgenland, WIGEV, AKH Wien's administrative backbone, and AUVA), leaving only OOe Gesundheitsholding and most of the independent Ordensspitaeler open.

Deployment-row philosophy follows this database's standard approach: a deployment row was created only where genuine cited evidence exists. For the ten hospital groups confirmed to run one vendor across every site (KAGes, NOe Landesgesundheitsagentur, SALK, Tirol Kliniken, KHBG, Gesundheit Burgenland, WIGEV, AUVA, Vinzenz Gruppe, Barmherzige Brueder Oesterreich), one deployment row was created per site rather than a single group-level row, since this report's table renderer resolves vendor status at site level (a dep_group_wide() bulk-payload helper, introduced during this pass, is now the standard pattern for future countries with a confirmed one-vendor-per-group structure). For NOe Landesgesundheitsagentur specifically, every Landesklinikum is recorded as CGM CLINICAL's confirmed contracted vendor per the group-wide 2019 award -- but only Universitaetsklinikum Neunkirchen has an independently confirmed live go-live date; the remaining 24 sites' actual rollout status is a live follow-up item. AKH Wien's SAP deployment is deliberately recorded as a carve_out: confirmed for the administrative/financial layer only, not the clinical-EPR scope covered by its separately-tracked, still-unconfirmed AKIM programme. Gesundheit Burgenland's SAP deployment is recorded as confirmed but with the specific module left unnamed in the product field, reflecting the indirect nature of its BI-case-study source. Two deployments remain explicitly unconfirmed: AKH Wien's AKIM clinical programme (vendor/product beyond the confirmed SAP administrative layer not named), and Kardinal Schwarzenberg Klinikum's TED-evidenced but unnamed clinical-information-system procurement.

Bed counts are still almost entirely unconfirmed (shown as n/k throughout) -- a WebSearch outage during this pass prevented a planned Statistik Austria bed-census lookup; this remains the clearest quick win for any further Austria research, matching the pattern already documented in this database's Sweden report.

OOe Gesundheitsholding remains the one major public hospital group wholly unconfirmed on core-KIS vendor despite a dedicated research effort this pass -- Oberoesterreich's largest operator (53.7% market share) showed only a clinical point-solution (Mint Medical's radiology tumour-diagnostics software at Kepler Universitaetsklinikum, TED-evidenced) and a "digital Fieberkurve" (fever chart) announcement describing integration into an unnamed existing KIS. Klinikum Wels-Grieskirchen and 9 of the report's 17 independent Ordensspitaeler (bundled under "Independent Ordensspitaeler") also remain unconfirmed; a per-institution web-research pass, rather than a further CPV sweep, is the recommended next step for these smaller private/religious sites, which rarely generate their own TED notices.

16. National health-IT governance: bodies and current leadership

Austria's federal structure splits hospital-IT-relevant governance across a national interoperability layer (ELGA GmbH), a national financing framework (BMSGPK, via 9 Landesgesundheitsfonds), and -- newly identified this pass as materially significant -- a national SAP user-group body (SAGA) coordinating the sector's response to the IS-H 2030 deadline. No single body plays the vendor-standardising role a Kanta (Finland) or MedCom (Denmark) plays; each Land's hospital operator makes its own KIS decision, exactly as this database found in Belgium. The table below sets out the bodies FHI will track as the national layer of the Austria market.

BMSGPK

Bundesministerium fuer Soziales, Gesundheit, Pflege und Konsumentenschutz

Federal ministry: sets national health policy and the hospital-financing framework legislation that underpins the 9 Landesgesundheitsfonds below.

ELGA GmbH

National eHealth/interoperability body (est. 2009): operates Austria's patient-facing electronic health record portal and the cross-system data-exchange layer every hospital connects to -- Austria's closest analogue to Finland's Kanta or Denmark's MedCom. Owned jointly by the federal government, all 9 Laender, and the umbrella social-insurance body. Does not standardise which KIS/EPR a hospital group runs -- an interoperability mandate only, the same pattern already documented in this database's Belgium report.

9 Landesgesundheitsfonds

One state health fund per Land (BURGEF, kgf, NOEGUS, OOe Gesundheitsfonds, SAGES, Gesundheitsfonds Steiermark, TGF, Vorarlberg's fund, Wiener Gesundheitsfonds) -- public-law bodies financing public nonprofit hospitals on a performance-based (LKF) system. Financing sits here; day-to-day hospital operation and IT procurement sits with each Land's separate hospital-operator company (KAGes, OOeG, KABEG, etc. -- see the sections below), the same funding/operating split already seen in Belgium's federal/regional divide.

Financing and operating roles are formally separate in every Land -- readers should not assume a Landesgesundheitsfonds itself chooses or funds a specific KIS vendor.

SAGA

SAP-Anwendergruppe Gesundheitswesen Oesterreich

Austrian SAP healthcare user group, coordinating the sector's response to SAP's 2030 IS-H end-of-support deadline -- the single most consequential national-level health-IT body this report identifies outside ELGA, given IS-H's estimated presence in 100+ Austrian hospitals.

17. Conclusions

Austria's EPR/KIS market gives this database its cleanest Land-level operating structure so far -- one public hospital operator per Bundesland -- and, after a same-day coverage-uplift pass, real vendor confirmations for 8 of 9 (all except OOe Gesundheitsholding). That research also surfaced this report's second major finding: CompuGroup Medical, not any single-Land specialist, is Austria's most widely-deployed KIS vendor by confirmed site count -- 37 of 116 mapped sites across Niederoesterreich, Vorarlberg and Wien, spread across three distinctly-branded products (CGM CLINICAL/NOEKIS, CGM Clinical, impuls.kis) that only resolve to one parent company on close inspection. But the market's dominant risk remains the one this report opened with: a single vendor platform, SAP IS-H, embedded in over 100 institutions across the public and private/religious sectors, now on a fixed retirement clock with under four years left on it and real doubt (49% of surveyed hospitals, 2023) about readiness -- a risk this pass extended further into AKH Wien's confirmed SAP administrative backbone and a tentative SAP link at Gesundheit Burgenland. For FHI's commercial purposes, the SAP deadline is plausibly the single highest-value near-term signal in this entire European database to date -- a forced, sector-wide replacement decision with a known deadline, not a speculative modernisation -- while CompuGroup Medical's quietly-dominant footprint is the more durable structural finding for competitive positioning.

Watch item: SAP's 2030 IS-H end-of-support deadline is the standing item to track across every future Austria pass -- specifically, whether the T-Systems Austria/ATSP S/4HANA adaptation approach reaches a proven, production-ready state, and which of this report's 100+ exposed institutions commit to it versus a full KIS replacement (which would itself become a major near-term procurement wave worth monitoring hospital-by-hospital).
Watch item: NOe Landesgesundheitsagentur's NOEKIS rollout, now roughly 4 years behind its original schedule with only 1 of 25 sites confirmed live -- the next 24 go-live dates are a concrete, trackable data point for this database's ongoing-monitoring phase, and a useful comparator against Sweden's Oracle Health Millennium collapse for how a large single-vendor hospital-IT contract can slip.
Watch item: Wiener Gesundheitsverbund's two 2025 market-research notices -- an explicit April 2025 "RFI Erneuerung KIS" followed by the broader November 2025 "Digital Patient Journey" notice -- are this report's strongest signal that Vienna's confirmed but ageing impuls.kis estate (live since 2010-2013) may be approaching a core-platform re-procurement. Worth checking for a formal tender in any follow-up pass: a WIGEV award would be one of this database's largest single Austrian contracts and could displace CompuGroup Medical's newly-discovered Vienna footprint.
Watch item: KAGes's unreconciled openMEDOCS-vs-SAP status is a priority research item, not just a methodological footnote -- if KAGes turns out to already be an IS-H site, Steiermark's entire 18-site network moves from this report's "confirmed but distinct" column into the sector-wide 2030 exposure count, materially changing the scale of the central finding above.
Watch item: OOe Gesundheitsholding is now the single highest-priority open item in this report -- Oberoesterreich's largest hospital operator by market share, with no core-KIS vendor confirmed despite two dedicated research passes. A per-hospital web-research sweep, rather than a further TED CPV sweep (which has now been tried twice without a direct hit for this group), is the recommended approach.
