Mapping the EPR landscape: hospital groups and standalone hospitals across Sweden

Sweden is nine-tenths of the way to a single-vendor EPR market — Cambio COSMIC now covers 17 of 21 regions live, with Stockholm and Gotland contracted to join by ~2029, taking the total to 19. But the remaining two regions carry the market’s most important live story: Skåne and Västra Götaland (VGR) both procured Oracle Health (Cerner) Millennium in 2016–18, and both have now abandoned it — VGR after a chaotic 3-day live rollout in November 2024 that produced 10 Lex Maria reports, Skåne without ever reaching a confirmed production go-live anywhere in the region. Together the two failed programmes cost roughly SEK 7.3bn (≈€658m) and left ~30% of Sweden’s population — 3.2 million people — on legacy Melior with no resolved future platform. This is one of the most significant EPR failures in recent European hospital IT, and it sits inside an otherwise unusually successful consolidation story.

Key findings

  • Cambio COSMIC is close to a national near-monopoly. 17 of 21 regions run COSMIC live today, via two joint-procurement consortia — the 8-region KGC (renewed Jan 2024) and the 9-region Sussa Samverkan (rollout completed Nov 2025). Stockholm and Gotland signed a further COSMIC contract in Nov 2025 (value SEK 2.3bn/12yr, ≈€207m), transitioning from legacy TakeCare with a target completion of ~2029 — taking eventual COSMIC coverage to 19 of 21 regions (90%).
  • Skåne and Västra Götaland both abandoned Oracle Health Millennium. VGR went live at Södra Älvsborgs Sjukhus (Borås) on 12 Nov 2024, suspended it after 3 days (10 Lex Maria reports filed), and formally terminated the contract 27 Aug 2026 for a SEK 657m (≈€59m) settlement on top of ~SEK 3.8bn (≈€343m) already invested. Skåne’s parallel Millennium programme (procured 2016-17, ~SEK 3.5bn /≈€316m sunk) never reached a confirmed go-live anywhere in the region — its designated pilot site, Lasarettet i Ystad, was repeatedly postponed before the whole programme was discontinued 29 Apr 2026.
  • Both abandoned-Millennium regions now run legacy Melior with no confirmed successor platform. VGR is pursuing a ~SEK 11bn (≈€992m) modular, multi-vendor rebuild with no fixed timeline; Skåne is evaluating three options (wait for Oracle’s next-generation Millennium, re-procure another monolithic system, or go modular) with a decision expected ~Oct 2026. Together these two regions cover ~3.2 million people (~30% of Sweden) currently without a resolved future EPR.
  • The Sussa Samverkan 2018 procurement award is contested. SVT Uppdrag Granskning (29-30 Apr 2026) reported that Cambio’s technical evaluation scores were raised at 11 points without documented justification, turning a losing bid into a 2-point win in a contract ultimately worth >SEK 4.3bn/10yr (≈€388m); a procurement law expert called it potentially unlawful. No reversal has been reported as of Sept 2026.
  • Several newly-migrated Sussa regions report patient-safety and performance concerns. Dalarna, Gävleborg and Blekinge have commissioned a joint external review (auditor Azets, due year-end 2026) after complaints spanning mixed-up patients/doses, extended outages and clinician-reported workflow problems; Västerbotten and Norrbotten show similar but less formalised post-launch friction. This is a standing FHI watch item across the newly-migrated cohort.
  • 96 hospital sites were mapped across all 21 regions and 73 hospital groups; bed counts are confirmed for a minority of sites (see §7 for the full market-share-by-beds coverage caveat) and six private-operator carve-outs (Capio and Aleris clinics co-located inside public hospitals) have an unconfirmed clinical system distinct from their host site’s regional default.

1. National context: a near-complete consolidation, with one major exception

Sweden’s public hospital system is organised around 21 self-governing regions (regioner), each of which owns its hospitals and has historically procured its own EPR independently — a fragmentation Statskontoret’s 2021 review of the failed “Vision e-hälsa 2025” national strategy called “a devastating structure for implementing new technology”. Two joint-procurement consortia have since consolidated most of that fragmentation onto a single vendor: the 8-region KGC (Kundgrupp Cosmic) — Uppsala, Östergötland, Jönköping, Kronoberg, Kalmar, Jämtland Härjedalen, Värmland and Västmanland, plus the private Capio S:t Görans Sjukhus in Stockholm — renewed its Cambio COSMIC contract 30 Nov 2023 (5yr + 3yr option); and the 9-region Sussa Samverkan — Örebro, Norrbotten, Västernorrland, Halland, Västerbotten, Dalarna, Sörmland, Gävleborg and Blekinge — jointly procured Cambio COSMIC in 2018 and completed its region-by-region rollout in Nov 2025, replacing a patchwork of NCS Cross, VAS and TakeCare deployments. Stockholm and Gotland signed a third, separate Cambio COSMIC contract in Nov 2025 (SEK 2.3bn/12yr, ≈€207m), migrating from legacy TakeCare on a phased 5-year rollout targeting ~2029.

Once that transition completes, Cambio COSMIC will run in 19 of 21 regions (90%) — a near-monopoly with no confirmed Epic presence anywhere in the country. The two regions outside every COSMIC consortium, Skåne and Västra Götaland, instead both independently contracted Oracle Health (Cerner) Millennium in 2016–18 — and both have now abandoned it, a story covered in full in §3 and §4 below.

2. Region Stockholm & Region Gotland: legacy TakeCare, transitioning to Cambio COSMIC

Stockholm and Gotland are treated together because they signed a single joint Cambio COSMIC contract in Nov 2025 and are migrating on the same timeline. Region Stockholm alone accounts for 2.49 million residents — Sweden’s largest region by population — and runs its hospitals on legacy TakeCare (itself a Cambio product), with one confirmed exception: Capio S:t Görans Sjukhus, Sweden’s first privately-owned acute hospital (1999, part of Ramsay Santé since 2018), is the sole private-hospital member of the KGC consortium and already runs Cambio COSMIC rather than TakeCare. The Nov 2025 Stockholm+Gotland contract (SEK 2.3bn/12yr, ≈€207m; total taxpayer cost estimated SEK 10–14bn, ≈€0.9–1.3bn, per press reporting, contested by losing bidders Systematic A/S and CGM before resolving Oct 2025) will bring the region’s remaining sites onto COSMIC by ~2029.

3. Region Västra Götaland (VGR): Melior, after a 3-day Oracle Health Millennium collapse

VGR (1.78 million residents, Sweden’s second-largest region) has long run Melior — an Oracle Health/Cerner-family EPR distinct from Oracle Health’s “Millennium” product — across its five hospital groups: Sahlgrenska Universitetssjukhuset (~1,600 beds group-wide), NU-sjukvården, Södra Älvsborgs Sjukhus (SÄS), Skaraborgs Sjukhus (SkaS) and Sjukhusen i väster (SiV). In 2017 VGR separately procured Oracle Health (Cerner) Millennium for a region-wide replacement (~SEK 5bn+, ≈€451m+). It went live at SÄS Borås plus Närhälsan primary-care clinics on 12 Nov 2024 05:30 and was suspended after three days (15 Nov 2024): 21 clicks to book an appointment, 45-minute tasks that should take 5 minutes, medication-list and prescription failures, radiology reverted to paper, patients diverted to Sahlgrenska, and 10 Lex Maria reports filed. A KPMG audit (~18 Mar 2025) recommended scrapping the programme for a modular rebuild; VGR’s regional board voted to abandon Millennium entirely on 2 Dec 2025, and a SEK 657m (≈€59m) termination settlement with Oracle was signed 27 Aug 2026, on top of ~SEK 3.8bn (≈€343m) already invested region-wide. Sahlgrenska, NU-sjukvården and SkaS never went live on Millennium at all — only SÄS Borås reached production, and only for three days.

4. Region Skåne: Melior + PMO, after Oracle Health Millennium was discontinued without ever going live

Skåne (1.43 million residents) runs Melior for hospital and specialist outpatient/inpatient care and the separate, non-interoperable PMO for all primary care, child health and habilitation/aids services, across eight public hospitals (SUS Lund+Malmö, Helsingborg, Ängelholm, Kristianstad, Hässleholm, Ystad, Trelleborg, Landskrona) plus the privately-operated Capio Närsjukhus Simrishamn. Like VGR, Skåne separately procured Oracle Health (Cerner) Millennium in 2016-17 (branded internally SDV, Skånes Digitala Vårdsystem) — but unlike VGR, no hospital in Skåne ever reached a confirmed production or pilot go-live. The designated pilot site, Lasarettet i Ystad, was repeatedly postponed (with no date set as of 21 Jan 2026) before the entire programme was discontinued by the regional board on 29 Apr 2026, after an internal usability review (ISO 9241-11) found the system not fit for purpose on efficiency and patient-safety grounds and lacking EU MDR CE certification. Total programme sunk cost: ~SEK 3.5bn / ≈€316m (2.3bn investment, 1.14bn consultants, 620.1m Cerner/Oracle fees, 838.8m internal staff). An inquiry has been opened into the original 2017 procurement decision after media reports of possible irregularities.

10. Data gaps, methodology and recommended next steps

This report was built from a single research pass across regional and hospital-group websites, Cambio and Inera press releases, SVT Nyheter and SVT Uppdrag Granskning, Läkartidningen, Region Skåne and VGR’s own board decisions and press releases, SCB (Statistiska centralbyrån), and the official sites of Sweden’s national health-IT governance bodies, gathered 1–2 September 2026. It is a strong first layer for the Sweden database, with structural gaps FHI should close before treating any record as complete:

  • Bed counts are confirmed for a minority of the 96 mapped sites — markedly thinner coverage than Denmark’s baseline (59%). Several confirmed figures are also dated (2017–2020) rather than current. A direct data request to each region’s capacity-planning office, or a pull from Vården i Siffror (vardenisiffror.se), is the most reliable way to close this gap with site-level precision.
  • Six private-operator carve-out sites have an unconfirmed EPR system: the three Capio tenants at Löwenströmska sjukhuset, the Aleris-operated clinic at Ängelholms sjukhus, the Capio specialistvård carve-out at Lasarettet i Motala, and Capio Närsjukhus Simrishamn. Direct outreach to Capio Sverige and Aleris Sverige head offices is the natural way to close this gap.
  • No dedicated TED (Tenders Electronic Daily) notice-ID sweep was performed for this edition — the five procurement notices in §9 are built from already-cited contract facts (press releases, board decisions) rather than formal award-notice IDs and values. A dedicated TED pass is a recommended follow-up, particularly for precise Millennium and COSMIC contract values.
  • Skåne’s and VGR’s replacement-platform decisions are both still open (Skåne expected ~Oct 2026; VGR’s modular rebuild has no fixed timeline) — this report reflects the abandoned-Millennium/legacy-Melior state as of Sept 2026 and should be revisited once either decision lands.

12. Conclusions

Sweden’s EPR market tells two stories at once. The first is a consolidation success: two multi-region joint-procurement consortia have taken a historically fragmented, 21-region market to a 90%-COSMIC near-monopoly (once Stockholm+Gotland completes by ~2029), with no confirmed Epic presence anywhere in the country. The second, playing out in parallel, is one of the most significant EPR procurement failures documented anywhere in this database.

The Oracle Health Millennium collapse: ~SEK 7.3bn (≈€658m) spent, ~30% of Sweden left without a resolved platform.

Skåne and Västra Götaland both independently contracted Oracle Health (Cerner) Millennium in 2016–18, outside every Cambio COSMIC consortium. VGR’s attempt reached production — for three days, at one hospital, before 10 Lex Maria reports forced a suspension and an eventual SEK 657m (≈€59m) termination settlement (27 Aug 2026) on top of ~SEK 3.8bn (≈€343m) already spent. Skåne’s attempt never reached production at all: nine years and ~SEK 3.5bn (≈€316m) after the original contract, the designated pilot site (Lasarettet i Ystad) had still not gone live when the regional board discontinued the programme outright on 29 Apr 2026. Both regions now run legacy Melior with no confirmed successor: VGR is pursuing an ~SEK 11bn (≈€992m) modular, multi-vendor rebuild with no fixed timeline, and Skåne is weighing three options with a decision expected ~Oct 2026. Together the two regions cover ~3.2 million people — roughly 30% of Sweden’s population — making this the largest open addressable EPR opportunity identified in the FHI database to date, and a case study in monolithic-EPR replacement risk that sits alongside (and arguably exceeds in severity) Denmark’s Sundhedsplatformen political-pressure story and the Netherlands’ UMCG contract dispute. FHI will track Skåne’s ~Oct 2026 decision, VGR’s modular-rebuild procurement activity, and the original 2017 Skåne procurement-integrity inquiry as the standing watch cluster for this market.

Separately, the Sussa Samverkan 2018 procurement’s contested technical-scoring allegations (§1, §6) and the joint Azets patient-safety review across three newly-migrated regions (due year-end 2026) are both live governance questions inside the otherwise-successful COSMIC consolidation, and worth tracking as a distinct line from the Skåne/VGR Millennium story above.
