Finland's EPR/EHR landscape: a five-vendor market reshaped by the 2023 wellbeing-county reform

Finland's 2023 health, social-services and rescue-services reform replaced 20 hospital districts and hundreds of municipal systems with 21 wellbeing services counties, the City of Helsinki, and the HUS Group specialised-care authority. Three years on, the resulting EPR/CPIS market is a genuine five-way contest -- Apotti (Epic), Tietoevry Care's Lifecare, CGI's OMNI360, the publicly-developed Esko, and Cambio COSMIC on autonomous Åland -- with Apotti itself now shrinking as three of its five founding Uusimaa owners have exited since May 2024.

Key findings

  • Apotti (Epic) has contracted sharply: Central, Western and Eastern Uusimaa all formally exited Oy Apotti Ab ownership on 2 May 2024 (combined exit cost ~€33m), leaving only HUS Group, the City of Helsinki and Vantaa & Kerava as owners (46.33% / 38.35% / 15.32%). HUS's own hospitals in the exiting counties' territory (Hyvinkää, Lohja, Porvoo) remain on Apotti regardless -- a hospital-level split that a county-level read of the market would miss entirely.
  • Tietoevry Care's Lifecare is now the single largest platform by county count -- roughly 14 of the 21 wellbeing counties either run it region-wide already or have selected it and are mid-rollout, from Kainuu's long-standing 2011 deployment to North Karelia's 2025 decision that will not go fully live until end 2027.
  • CGI's OMNI360 has become the second commercial platform of scale, procured by Central Uusimaa (Keusote), Pirkanmaa and North Savo (joint tender, 4 Apr 2024) and Southwest Finland/Varha (22 Mar 2024) -- but in every one of these regions specialised hospital care (Tays, KYS, Tyks) has not yet gone live, running on legacy systems while primary care operates on OMNI360.
  • The publicly-developed Esko system, built in North Ostrobothnia (Pohde), is the only non-commercial platform with real scale -- used in specialised care at Oulu University Hospital for decades and now the intended (but not yet complete) unification target for Lapland, which itself inherited roughly 300 separate legacy patient-record systems at the 2023 reform.
  • Åland sits entirely outside this picture: as an autonomous region outside the mainland reform, its health authority (ÅHS) runs Cambio COSMIC -- Sweden's most common hospital EPR -- awarded May 2024, with Swedish-language UNA Oy consent/forms modules layered on top from September 2025.
  • Evidence quality is markedly uneven: Kymenlaakso and South Karelia's vendor relationships are confirmed via official document-publicity descriptions (Tier 1), while several counties (Päijät-Häme's vendor, and exact go-live years for South and Central Ostrobothnia's long-standing Lifecare deployments) rest on a single trade-press roundup and are flagged as needing primary-source confirmation.

1. National context: 21 counties, one capital, one shared hospital authority

Finland's 1 January 2023 health, social services and rescue services reform (sote-uudistus) dissolved the country's 20 hospital districts and hundreds of separate municipal social/health organisations, replacing them with 21 new wellbeing services counties (hyvinvointialueet). Two entities sit outside that structure by constitutional exception: the City of Helsinki, which continues to organise its own primary and social care, and the autonomous, Swedish-speaking Åland Islands, which run their own health authority (ÅHS) entirely separately from the mainland reform.

A third structural layer is specific to the capital region: the HUS Group (HUS-yhtymä), a specialised-care joint authority jointly owned by Helsinki and the four Uusimaa wellbeing counties (Vantaa & Kerava, Central, Western and Eastern Uusimaa), which operates most acute hospitals across the region -- Meilahti, Töölö, the New Children's Hospital, the Women's Hospital, Jorvi, Peijas, Hyvinkää, Lohja and Porvoo -- regardless of which wellbeing county the hospital happens to sit in geographically. This matters directly for EPR data: as the Uusimaa section below sets out, three of the four Uusimaa wellbeing counties have exited Apotti for their own primary/social-care systems, but HUS's own hospitals inside their territory have not moved and remain on Apotti -- a hospital-level fact that a county-level summary of "who uses what" would get wrong.

Wellbeing counties are centrally funded by the state and, unlike municipalities, cannot yet levy their own taxes -- a constraint several counties' 2025-2026 financial pressure (see South Karelia's formal state financial-oversight procedure, §8 below) suggests is starting to bind on IT investment timelines as much as anything else.

2. Uusimaa: Apotti's shrinking core and three counties that left

Uusimaa is Finland's most populous region and the origin of Apotti, the Epic-based system built as a joint venture between Helsinki, HUS and the surrounding municipalities from 2013. As of September 2026, Apotti's ownership has narrowed to three parties -- HUS Group (46.33%), the City of Helsinki (38.35%) and Vantaa & Kerava (15.32%) -- after Central Uusimaa (Keusote), Western Uusimaa and Eastern Uusimaa all formally exited ownership on 2 May 2024, at a combined exit cost of roughly €33m (€11m / €17m / €5m respectively). Each departing county has since procured its own replacement: Keusote selected CGI's OMNI360 (~€10m, consolidating six municipalities' separate systems), while Western and Eastern Uusimaa jointly selected Tietoevry Care's Lifecare (Tietoevry scored 100/100 against CGI's 86.71 in the evaluation), now rolling out in phases through 2026.

Crucially, none of this touches HUS Group's own hospitals. HUS still runs Apotti at Jorvi (Espoo, within Western Uusimaa's territory), Hyvinkää (within Keusote's territory) and Porvoo (within Eastern Uusimaa's territory) -- the specialised-care sites the exiting counties don't themselves operate. The practical result for 2026 is a genuinely split landscape inside a single county's borders: a resident of Western Uusimaa may have their GP record in Lifecare and their hospital record in Apotti, at different sites of the same visit pathway. In March 2026, HUS, Helsinki and Vantaa & Kerava jointly commissioned consultancy BearingPoint to study whether to replace Apotti altogether once Epic's underlying contract term expires in 2026 -- described by HUS's own administrative director as realistic only "in the late 2030s" given the system's depreciation schedule, so this is exploratory rather than a near-term change.

3. Southwest Finland (Varha): CGI OMNI360 replacing legacy Uranus

Southwest Finland (Varsinais-Suomen hyvinvointialue, branded Varha) is Finland's second-largest wellbeing county by population (~498,000, 27 municipalities), centred on Turku University Hospital (Tyks). CGI's OMNI360 was selected 22 March 2024 to replace the region's legacy Uranus system, run in parallel with near-identical procurements at Pirkanmaa and North Savo. Primary and dental care are already live; Tyks's specialised hospital care is targeted for December 2026 and had not gone live as of this report.

6. Pirkanmaa: OMNI360 live in primary care, Tays specialist go-live delayed

19. Lapland: Esko as the intended platform, but ~300 inherited systems and a decade to unify

Lapland is this report's clearest case of a wellbeing county whose EPR landscape the 2023 reform has not yet resolved. Running two acute hospitals -- Lapland Central Hospital in Rovaniemi and Länsi-Pohjan sairaala in Kemi (the former separate Länsi-Pohja hospital district, merged in at the reform) -- the county inherited roughly 300 separate patient-record systems from its constituent municipalities and former hospital districts. Esko is the intended unification platform, run through the "Tokka" project, but full consolidation is projected to take around ten years; a physician survey cited by Yle rated Apotti the lowest-scoring of twelve platforms compared nationally, for context, but Apotti itself is not in use in Lapland. This report records Lapland's Esko relationship as confirmed as the intended platform but unconfirmed as complete coverage -- a carve-out-heavy region rather than a single clean deployment.

23. Methodology and evidentiary notes

This report follows FHI's standard eight-step per-country method: national structure research, site and leadership enumeration, a procurement sweep, a deployment pass restricted to core categories with wall-to-wall-then-carve-out logic, explicit gap flagging, local validation against the underlying database's CHECK constraints before import, report generation from the same payload used for the live database, and a beds/cover/region-map/governance layer built in from the start (this is Finland's first report, so nothing here is a retrofit).

Two structural features of the Finnish market required departures from the pattern used in prior country reports. First, hospital-level vendor status can differ from wellbeing-county-level status in the Uusimaa region specifically, because HUS Group operates hospitals inside host counties that have themselves moved to a different vendor for their own primary/social-care systems -- every HUS-operated site in this report is flagged with its host county's own status alongside its own. Second, a "confirmed" vendor relationship does not imply a live deployment in several regions (North Karelia, and the specialised-care layer at Pirkanmaa, North Savo, Southwest Finland and North Ostrobothnia) -- each such row states the go-live timeline explicitly in its notes rather than leaving "confirmed" to imply completion.

The national map uses pre-2023 maakunta (traditional region) boundaries from the varmais/maakunnat public dataset, not 2023-era hyvinvointialue boundaries -- click_that_hood, this project's usual boundary source, has no Finland regions file (checked and confirmed absent this pass). The practical effect is a single Uusimaa polygon standing in for four separate 2023 wellbeing counties plus HUS Group; the map is coloured by Apotti as the dominant vendor for that reason (see §2), and every hospital-site marker on top of it is coloured by that individual site's own confirmed vendor rather than the regional generalisation.

A number of individual facts are flagged unconfirmed or noted as resting on a single secondary source throughout this report -- most significantly Päijät-Häme's EPR vendor and CEO (the county's own site blocked repeated fetch attempts), several satellite-site street addresses (Tyks Loimaa, Tays Valkeakoski/Sastamala/Pitkäniemi, Iisalmi, Varkaus, Hamina, Karhula), and the exact go-live years for South and Central Ostrobothnia's long-running Lifecare deployments. These are recommended follow-up items rather than omissions.

24. National health-IT governance: bodies and current leadership

Three national bodies sit above the 21 wellbeing counties' individual EPR/CPIS choices, plus the HUS Group as the capital region's own specialised-care authority: the Ministry of Social Affairs and Health (STM) sets national digital-health policy and funds the reform; the Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare (THL) sets the interoperability and coding standards every vendor's system must meet; and Kanta Services, run by the national benefits agency Kela, is the mandatory national health information exchange layer that every regional system -- Apotti, Lifecare, OMNI360 and Esko alike -- must integrate with, meaning no county-level vendor choice can opt out of national interoperability the way some Nordic neighbours' regions can. The table below sets out the bodies FHI will track as the national layer of the Finland market, current as of September 2026.

25. Conclusions

Three years after Finland's most significant health-system reorganisation in a generation, its EPR/CPIS market has settled into a genuine five-way contest rather than consolidating around one platform -- and the reform itself is still actively reshaping that contest, not merely having set its starting conditions. Apotti, the system the reform inherited as Uusimaa's incumbent, is now smaller than it was at the reform's start, while Tietoevry's Lifecare has become the de facto national default for wellbeing counties without a strong existing system to defend. CGI's OMNI360 has real momentum in primary care but a live, tested track record at zero of its four regions' main hospitals as of this report -- worth watching closely through 2026 as Tyks, Tays and KYS approach their delayed specialist-care go-lives. Esko remains the most interesting structural outlier: a publicly-developed platform with real scale at one of Finland's five university hospitals, now the explicit target for unifying one of the country's most fragmented regions.

Watch item: the HUS/Helsinki/Vantaa-Kerava BearingPoint study into replacing Apotti entirely (reported March 2026) is exploratory only, with HUS's own administrative director describing a realistic replacement horizon in the late 2030s given the system's depreciation schedule -- but any signal of that timeline shortening would be a first-order event for the whole Nordic Epic footprint, not just Finland.
Watch item: North Karelia's Lifecare rollout (selected Oct 2025, not fully live until end 2027) and Pirkanmaa/North Savo/Southwest Finland/North Ostrobothnia's still-pending specialist-care go-lives mean a meaningful share of this report's "confirmed" deployments will change status -- to fully live, or conceivably to abandoned, as Sweden's Region Skåne and VGR Millennium projects were -- well within the next 12-18 months. This report should be refreshed against primary sources at that horizon rather than treated as a static snapshot.
