Belgium's EPR/EHR landscape: 25 hospital networks, seven vendors, and a market where the network map is not the vendor map

Belgium's 2019 hospital-network reform grouped every acute hospital into one of up to 25 loco-regional clinical networks -- 13 in Flanders, 8 touching Wallonia, and 3 confirmed in Brussels -- but, unlike the wellbeing-county reforms this database has already mapped in Finland and Sweden, network membership in Belgium carries no obligation to share an EPR/CPIS platform. The result, confirmed repeatedly in this report's own hospital-level data, is a seven-vendor market split roughly along linguistic-community lines: Nexuzhealth (Flanders' widest footprint, not Epic-derived, correcting an initial working assumption), UZ Brussel's own Primuz, ChipSoft's HiX at Antwerp's ZAS and AZ Delta, Zorgi's Xperthis CARE dominant across Wallonia's public intercommunales (also not Agfa/ORBIS-derived, a second correction), Epic at Brussels' Cliniques universitaires Saint-Luc, and Oracle Health/Cerner at three Flemish university-linked hospitals.

Key findings

  • Two working assumptions from this project's original research brief were overturned during sourcing and corrected before reaching the database: Nexuzhealth is not an Epic-based platform -- it commercialises UZ Leuven's own home-grown "Klinisch Werkstation" (KWS), developed as a 2016 Cegeka/UZ Leuven joint venture -- and Xperthis (rebranded Zorgi in 2025/26) has no historical link to Agfa HealthCare/ORBIS; its lineage runs through NRB Group's 2011 Polymedis/Partezis/Xtenso merger and 2015 MIMS/Ciges acquisitions, on an InterSystems Caché database.
  • Network membership does not imply a shared EPR: Ziekenhuisnetwerk Gent alone spans three vendors across its four members (Oracle Health/Cerner at UZ Gent and AZ Sint-Lucas Gent, Primuz at AZ Jan Palfijn Gent, unconfirmed at AZ Oudenaarde), and CUROZ splits between UZ Brussel's own Primuz and Nexuzhealth at its Flemish-Brabant partner hospitals -- a pattern independently corroborated by a 2021 Antares HealthProspecting analysis and visible throughout this report's per-network tables.
  • Nexuzhealth has the single widest confirmed footprint in this database -- 17 of 39 confirmed/unconfirmed deployment records, spanning nine of Flanders' 13 networks -- built through two identifiable waves of hospital adoption (the January 2018 "5 nieuwe ziekenhuizen" announcement was itself a second wave following earlier 2017 go-lives) rather than a single coordinated rollout.
  • Wallonia's EPR market is comparatively concentrated around Zorgi's Xperthis CARE at the public intercommunales that have confirmed a vendor (Grand Hôpital de Charleroi, Centre de Santé des Fagnes, CHR Sambre et Meuse, Clinique Notre-Dame de Grâce, Clinique Saint-Luc Bouge, Hôpitaux Iris Sud) -- but the region's single largest hospital by network size, CHU de Liège, runs an in-house-branded system ("HORUS") whose underlying commercial vendor this research could not establish, and Réseau Move's three hospitals (spanning Wallonia and the German-speaking Community) were, as of an August 2023 EU tender, still jointly procuring a first common DPI with no confirmed award outcome.
  • Evidence quality is markedly uneven and split roughly along the federal fault line this report also documents: Flanders' vendor confirmations mostly trace to named hospital/vendor press coverage (Tier 4-6), Wallonia's strongest confirmations come from a federal hospital-bed-planning PDF plus a handful of hospital-published DPI announcements, and Brussels' CHORUS network -- the largest in the country by site count -- has a confirmed vendor at precisely one of its 18 mapped sites (Cliniques universitaires Saint-Luc's Epic, outside CHORUS, in the separate H.uni network); every other CHORUS site's EPR remains unconfirmed despite a strong circumstantial lead (H.U.B. group CIO Jacques Rossler previously led IT at both Saint-Luc and Erasme) that this report explicitly declines to treat as fact.

1. National context: a federal financing split, a 25-network reform, and a market the reform did not consolidate

Health policy in Belgium is split unusually finely across levels of government, and that split matters directly for how EPR/CPIS decisions get made and funded. Hospitals' day-to-day operational financing (the BMF/BFM budget) runs through the federal level via INAMI/RIZIV, while since the Sixth State Reform of 2014 infrastructure and capital investment -- the budget line an EPR replacement typically falls under -- is a Region/Community competence: Departement Zorg in Flanders, AVIQ in Wallonia, Vivalis (formerly Iriscare) via the COCOM in Brussels, and a joint role for the German-speaking Community on the one hospital network it co-accredits. No single Belgian body plays the role Kanta (Finland) or MedCom (Denmark) play as a mandatory national exchange layer with direct funding leverage over vendor choice -- the federal eHealth-platform sets interoperability standards and operates the exchange infrastructure, but the vendor decision itself sits with each hospital or hospital group.

The federal Act of 28 February 2019 required every Belgian general/acute hospital to join exactly one loco-regional clinical network from 1 January 2020, targeting roughly 25 networks nationally: a statutory maximum of 13 in Flanders (all 13 reached), 8 touching Wallonia (7 Walloon-only plus Réseau Move, jointly accredited with the German-speaking Community), and up to 4 in Brussels, of which only 3 -- CHORUS, H.uni and CUROZ -- were confirmed as constituted in this research pass. Critically, network membership is a clinical-coordination obligation, not an IT-procurement one: this report's own hospital-level data repeatedly shows sister hospitals in the same network running different, unrelated EPR platforms, a pattern independently corroborated by a 2021 Antares HealthProspecting market analysis. Readers used to this database's Nordic country reports, where a region's system is usually a genuinely shared regional decision, should not carry that assumption into Belgium.

2. Flanders: 13 networks, Nexuzhealth's home ground, and a genuine multi-vendor market

Flanders reached its statutory maximum of 13 loco-regional networks. Two structurally distinct "networks" coexist in the region and should not be conflated: the 13 mandatory clinical networks covered here, and the separate, voluntary Vlaams Ziekenhuisnetwerk (VZN) knowledge-sharing network founded in 2002 (now 32 hospitals, closely associated with the Nexuzhealth/Klinisch Werkstation platform), which Ziekenhuisnetwerk Gent joined as a group on 6 June 2025. Four vendors have real scale in Flanders -- Nexuzhealth (widest footprint), Primuz (UZ Brussel's own platform, extended to several Flemish partner hospitals), ChipSoft's HiX (Antwerp's ZAS combine and AZ Delta), and Oracle Health/Cerner (UZA, UZ Gent, AZ Sint-Lucas Gent, announced together in 2017) -- with roughly half the region's hospital sites still carrying no confirmed vendor in this pass.

3. Ziekenhuisnetwerk Gent: three vendors inside one network

Ziekenhuisnetwerk Gent is this report's clearest single illustration of the network-does-not-mean-vendor finding: within its four members, UZ Gent and AZ Sint-Lucas Gent both run Oracle Health/Cerner (UZ Gent's instance branded "Geniuz", chosen 2017 to replace a 1997-era system), AZ Jan Palfijn Gent runs Primuz (live February 2020, implemented by CTG Health Solutions over a two-year phased rollout), and AZ Oudenaarde's vendor remains unconfirmed despite the hospital's own site confirming participation in the COZO regional health-data-sharing platform. The network itself joined VZN as a group on 6 June 2025.

25. Brussels-Capital Region: three confirmed networks, one confirmed vendor

Brussels' network formation was more complex and delayed than Flanders' or Wallonia's, reflecting the region's tri-partite governance (Flemish Community, French Community, and bicommunal COCOM). The regulatory ceiling allows up to 4 networks; this research confirms only three constituted -- CHORUS (the largest hospital network in Belgium by site count), H.uni (the UCLouvain/Saint-Luc-affiliated group), and CUROZ (UZ Brussel's own, Flemish-Community-aligned network) -- with no fourth network identified. Brussels is, evidentially, this report's weakest region: of 27 mapped hospital sites across the three networks, only Cliniques universitaires Saint-Luc's Epic deployment is confirmed with primary sourcing.

31. Methodology and evidentiary notes

This report follows FHI's standard eight-step per-country method: national structure research, site and leadership enumeration (split across four parallel regional research passes for Flanders, Wallonia, Brussels and a national/vendor cross-reference), a procurement sweep, a deployment pass, explicit gap-flagging, a dedicated reconciliation pass to resolve cross-file conflicts, local validation against the underlying database's CHECK constraints before import, and report generation from the same payload used for the live database.

Two structural corrections to this project's original working assumptions were made during sourcing, before reaching the database, and are flagged prominently here because they would otherwise have propagated an error into every subsequent reference to these vendors. First, Nexuzhealth is not Epic-based: it is a 2016 joint venture between Cegeka and UZ Leuven commercialising UZ Leuven's own home-grown "Klinisch Werkstation" platform, not a US Epic implementation. Second, Xperthis (rebranded Zorgi in 2025/26) has no historical link to Agfa HealthCare or its ORBIS product: its actual lineage runs through NRB Group's 2011 merger of Polymedis, Partezis and Xtenso, plus 2015 acquisitions of MIMS (OmniPro DPI) and Ciges, running on an InterSystems Caché database -- a genuinely separate, unrelated Agfa HealthCare ORBIS deployment does exist historically in Belgium (AZ Monica, 2006; Heilig Hartziekenhuis Mol, 2007) but is recorded in this database as its own distinct, largely legacy relationship, not as evidence for an Agfa-Xperthis connection.

A third finding shaped this report's whole structure rather than one vendor entry: hospital-network membership does not imply a shared EPR/CPIS platform in Belgium, unlike the wellbeing-county reforms this database has mapped in Finland and Sweden. This is independently corroborated by a 2021 Antares HealthProspecting market analysis and is visible directly in this report's own data (Ziekenhuisnetwerk Gent's three vendors, §3; CUROZ's Primuz/Nexuzhealth split, §28). Because of this, the national map (§facts, above) fills each province by region (Flanders/Wallonia/Brussels) rather than by dominant vendor -- the pattern used for Finland and Sweden's maps, where a single vendor genuinely does dominate most regions, does not fit Belgium's data and would have overstated a false regional consistency.

Deployment-row philosophy for this report deliberately departs from the more liberal placeholder pattern used for Sweden: a deployment row was created only where genuine cited vendor evidence exists, even where that evidence is lower-tier (a single trade-press mention, a portal-branding signal such as AZ Diest's "nexuzhealthpro" login). No "Unknown/unconfirmed" placeholder rows were manufactured for hospital sites lacking any vendor evidence -- those sites simply carry no deployment record, meaning they exist and are mapped, but their vendor status is genuinely open rather than asserted-then-flagged. A small number of exceptions are recorded explicitly as status: unconfirmed deployments because a specific, named (if uncertain, or not yet live) claim exists: AZ Monica's historical Agfa HealthCare ORBIS reference; CHU de Liège's HORUS system, whose underlying commercial vendor could not be established despite it being one of Wallonia's flagship hospitals (and which a Sep 2026 pass shows is now the subject of a live, network-wide replacement tender -- see §19); and Clinique Saint-Jean's three sites, documented via an official ex-ante procurement notice as extending Saint-Luc's Epic contract but not yet live (§27). Coverage also distinguishes a carve_out status from full EPR/PAS coverage where the evidence is specifically scoped -- CHR de la Citadelle's confirmed ICU/critical-care PDMS (Itémedical MetaVision, §19) does not imply anything about its hospital-wide platform, which remains separately unconfirmed.

A Sep 2026 coverage-uplift pass, prompted directly by Jon's review of this report, closed two research-tooling gaps flagged in the original pass and materially improved site-level coverage. TED's own Search API (api.ted.europa.eu/v3/notices/search, a documented, public, unauthenticated REST endpoint) turned out to be entirely reachable -- the earlier finding that TED was "inaccessible" reflected a network-egress restriction specific to this research environment's default sandbox, not a limitation of TED itself; routing the same queries through a real browser session resolved it completely. A systematic sweep on CPV 48814000 ("medical information systems") for Belgian buyers returned 57 notices and directly produced five new or upgraded confirmations (AZ Turnhout, AZ Damiaan's evidence upgraded to tier 2, CHR de la Citadelle's ICU carve-out, Réseau Helora network-wide via CHU Tivoli's integration notice, and Clinique Saint-Jean's pending Epic extension) plus the live Réseau Elipse network-wide tender and the CHwapi patient-flow tender -- taking confirmed-or-pending site coverage from 39 to 50 deployment records across 49 of 144 sites. publicprocurement.be was confirmed to be a genuine public, no-login-required JavaScript application (not access-restricted) that simply cannot be read by a plain HTTP fetch; a proper interactive pass through it, covering Belgium's sub-EU-threshold notices, remains a follow-up item. COCOF (Commission communautaire française) is now resolved rather than open: cocof.be was simply a dead domain (the body now operates as ccf.brussels), and, per its own site and a secondary source on the Brussels health-competence split, COCOF holds no material hospital-IT-relevant competence -- its residual role is limited to subsidy distribution to monocommunal francophone health institutions, a role the 2013 Sainte-Émilie agreement largely migrated to COCOM, which this database already tracks as Vivalis. No Brussels hospital network in this report's data is affected by a COCOF-specific competence.

32. National health-IT governance: bodies and current leadership

Six bodies sit above the 24 hospital networks' individual EPR/CPIS choices, split across the federal/regional financing divide set out in §1: the federal eHealth-platform and INAMI/RIZIV on the operational and interoperability side, and Departement Zorg, AVIQ, Vivalis/COCOM and the German-speaking Community holding the regional infrastructure-financing and network-accreditation competences that actually touch EPR procurement decisions. 33. Conclusions

Belgium's EPR/CPIS market resists the single organising narrative this database has been able to draw for its Nordic country reports. There is no reform moment analogous to Finland's 2023 wellbeing-county consolidation or Sweden's regional-council structure that ties network membership to platform choice -- instead, Belgium's 25-network reform solved a clinical-coordination governance problem while leaving IT procurement exactly where it already sat, with each hospital or hospital group. The practical result, mapped hospital by hospital in this report, is a market that splits more along linguistic-community and ownership lines than along the network map itself: Nexuzhealth's Flemish, Epic-independent dominance; Zorgi's Xperthis CARE consolidating steadily across Wallonia's public intercommunales; ChipSoft's HiX concentrated but very large at Antwerp's ZAS; and Brussels split three ways with by far this report's thinnest evidence base. The two working-assumption corrections made during this research pass -- Nexuzhealth's true Cegeka/UZ Leuven origin, and Xperthis's true NRB/KEYES lineage rather than an Agfa/ORBIS one -- matter beyond this single report: both vendors recur across every subsequent EU country this database will map, and both errors, uncorrected, would have propagated into every future cross-country vendor comparison FHI produces.

Watch item: Réseau Elipse's open, network-wide DPI tender (TED 379038-2026, dispatched by CHU de Liège via SPF Stratégie et Appui, submission deadline 15 Jul 2026, no award yet as of this pass) is the single largest live procurement signal in this report -- a successful award would resolve, in one contract, the vendor status of up to 8 member institutions and 12 sites, including the network's flagship, CHU de Liège, whose existing "HORUS" system this tender is evidently intended to replace. This should be the first item checked in any follow-up pass on Belgium.
Watch item: Réseau Move's joint DPI tender (TED 405372-2023, closed August 2023, no confirmed award) is exactly the kind of live signal this database's ongoing-monitoring phase should track -- three hospitals spanning two Regions/Communities selecting their first shared modern EPR would be a genuinely new data point, not an update to an existing one.
Watch item: Xperthis/Zorgi's 2025/26 rebrand and its merger with Infohos Solutions, alongside NRB Group's own rebrand to KEYES (24-25 April 2026), both occurred within the research window for this report -- any further corporate restructuring at this vendor should be checked before this report's vendor-landscape section is treated as current beyond the next 12 months.
Watch item: HUmani Santé Charleroi Chimay's reported 2025 insolvency risk, alongside its governance disruption following a bribery investigation, is material context for any commercial IT-investment assessment of that account -- an intercommunale under financial and governance stress is a plausible candidate for delayed or cancelled EPR investment, worth monitoring directly rather than assuming continuity with CSF Chimay's confirmed Xperthis platform.
